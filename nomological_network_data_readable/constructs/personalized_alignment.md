# Personalized alignment
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Personalized preference alignment, Personal preference alignment  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the prevailing usage treats personalized alignment as a latent model ability to adapt or tailor outputs to user preferences. In PAD: Personalized Alignment of LLMs at Decoding-time, this is framed as matching diverse personalized preferences that vary across cultural, educational, and political differences, while FaST: Feature-aware Sampling and Tuning for Personalized Preference Alignment with Limited Data narrows the target to the specific preferences of a particular user. A closely matching formulation, personal preference alignment, likewise defines it as generating outputs that reflect individual user preferences, with those preferences inferred from comparative response evaluations.

**Space of facets.** The construct appears under three closely related names, personalized alignment, personalized preference alignment, and personal preference alignment. Across these labels, papers use it for much the same latent ability, but they differ in scope: some descriptions foreground diverse preferences across user groups, while others center a particular user's individual preferences, and one definition makes the preference signal explicit by tying it to comparative response evaluations.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow in the available papers: the construct is linked to 1 measurement instrument, HelpSteer2, and to no behavioral indicators. The only recorded instrument is used as a dataset-level measure through "alignment results on HelpSteer2 dataset," so the available measurement evidence stays at the level of overall alignment results rather than separate behavioral indicators.
