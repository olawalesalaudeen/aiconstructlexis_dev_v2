# Persuasiveness
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  
**Also known as:** Persuasion  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the cited definitions, the prevailing framing treats persuasiveness or persuasion as how much an argument can convince or influence others. One definition states this as the effectiveness of generated arguments across contexts, captured as "Effectiveness of argument across contexts," while another frames it as a latent capacity of an argument or participant to influence others' opinions and connects it to linguistic markers and turn-taking dynamics. The shared core is audience influence, with the main difference being whether papers describe it as observed argumentative effectiveness or as a latent capacity attached to an argument or participant.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not lay out a detailed subtype scheme for this construct. The main variation is terminological and in the unit of analysis: one paper uses persuasiveness for a dimension of generated arguments, while another uses persuasion for a property of either an argument or a participant. That second usage also places it alongside argument quality, describing persuasion as an indicator of argument quality and as a proxy for identifying markers of whether facilitator intervention is needed.

**Space of measurement.** Its operationalization is narrow, with 2 linked measurement instruments, Human evaluation and LLM-as-a-judge. In the argumentative writing setting, persuasiveness is one of five evaluation dimensions, scored with a GPT-4o-based LLM-as-a-judge and also with human evaluation using the same dimensions. This operationalization matches definitions that emphasize convincing or influencing an audience; the only linked behavioral indicator, Information extraction, is not used as a measure.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, one paper claims a causal relationship from persuasiveness to Information extraction, stating that interviewer-LLMs struggle with engaging persuasively, "leading to suboptimal information extraction across model size and capability." The cited papers do not report correlational or other related links for this construct.
