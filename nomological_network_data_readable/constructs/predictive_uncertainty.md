# Predictive uncertainty
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 13 papers  
**Also known as:** Prediction uncertainty, Model uncertainty  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define predictive uncertainty as the model's uncertainty or lack of confidence in its output predictions, often next-token predictions and often operationalized with output-distribution entropy rather than parameter uncertainty. A few papers narrow this to specific settings such as memorized sequences, iterative reasoning, or preference comparisons, but they are still studying the same core property of the model's predictions.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage describes predictive uncertainty as the model's lack of confidence or ambiguity in its predictions or generated outputs, usually at the next-token level and often quantified through the entropy of the output distribution, with higher entropy marking greater uncertainty. Some papers present it dynamically, as how quickly generation moves from initial uncertainty to confident predictions, especially when prefixes are ambiguous or complex, and a smaller line applies the same idea to preference predictions near the decision boundary.

**Space of facets.** Papers use the labels predictive uncertainty, prediction uncertainty, and sometimes model uncertainty for closely related notions centered on output-side prediction confidence. A recurring distinction is between predictive uncertainty over outputs and weight uncertainty over parameters, and one paper says predictive uncertainty can be decomposed into different sources. Some authors also frame reasoning or self-training as processes that progressively reduce prediction uncertainty.

**Space of measurement.** No named measurement instrument is linked to it, and one linked behavioral indicator appears, Hallucination. Papers instead operationalize the construct through direct analyses of model outputs, most often with entropy of the predictive distribution or predictive entropy, including next-token entropy, maximizing predictive entropy, and treating predictions closest to 0.5 as most uncertain in preference modeling; one study also tracks how quickly and stably generation shifts from high ambiguity to confident predictions, and another uses an entropy threshold to decide when to intervene. These operationalizations mostly match the dominant definition as uncertainty or confidence in predictions, though some apply the same logic to preference predictions rather than standard next-token generation.

**Links.** The only recurring linked phenomenon is Hallucination. One paper claims a causal relationship from Predictive uncertainty to Hallucination, stating that when the model is uncertain about the semantic meaning of the next tokens to generate, it is likely to start hallucinating, and another paper reports that Hallucination is correlated with Predictive uncertainty through a strong positive correlation with model uncertainty.
