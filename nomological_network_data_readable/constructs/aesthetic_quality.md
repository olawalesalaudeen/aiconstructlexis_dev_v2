# Aesthetic quality
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Design quality  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define aesthetic quality as the overall visual appeal or artistic quality of a visual output, such as an image or banner, often illustrated with features like color harmony, composition, layout, style, and typography. One paper phrases it as a presentation's ability to achieve harmonious colors and proper layout that improve readability and engagement, but it is still the same core property of how visually pleasing the artifact is.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats aesthetic quality as the overall visual appeal of a generated visual artifact, sometimes explicitly adding artistic merit or overall quality. Most definitions unpack that appeal through features such as color harmony, layout or composition, typography, style, and similar visual elements. A smaller line of work uses the nearby label design quality for a presentation's latent ability to show harmonious colors, proper layout, and visually appealing elements that enhance readability and engagement, so the main difference is between an output-focused description of visual appeal and a latent design-capability framing.

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly organize the construct by listing facets rather than by naming formal subtypes. Recurring facets include color harmony, layout balance or proper layout, typography, composition, overall style, artistic merit, and overall design quality, and one definition also ties the visual properties to readability and engagement. The terminology is not fully uniform: most papers say aesthetic quality, while one uses design quality for a closely overlapping description, and some papers discuss it alongside semantic consistency, relevance, or user preferences as a separate target.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to this construct, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The papers nevertheless sometimes use score-based or reward-based wording when talking about assessment, for example describing an Aesthetic Quality Score as measuring visual appeal through color harmony, layout balance, typography, and overall design quality, or referring to public aesthetic rewards that reflect aesthetic appeal. No linked construct is recorded for it.
