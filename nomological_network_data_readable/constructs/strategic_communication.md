# Strategic communication
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Language communication  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** In the available usage, strategic communication is framed as a latent ability to use natural language effectively for persuasion, deception, coordination, and information sharing in strategic interactions. The same paper places it in free-form conversation used to achieve agreements and perform strategic deceptions, and illustrates it with a voting coordination strategy in which a Seer asks Villagers to vote for a strongly suspected Werewolf.

**Space of facets.** The only explicit breakdown treats the construct as spanning persuasion, deception, coordination, and information sharing. The snippets further emphasize agreement seeking, strategic deception, and vote coordination, but no additional taxonomy, synonym, or contrast with other constructs is stated.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to it. The available evidence instead illustrates the construct through in-game language behavior, including free-form conversation for agreements and deception and an explicit request that Villagers coordinate their votes.

**Links.** One paper claims a causal relationship from strategic reasoning to strategic communication, stating that iterative alternation in LSPO agents progressively enhances both strategic reasoning and language communication. No correlational or other related construct links are reported beyond this connection.
