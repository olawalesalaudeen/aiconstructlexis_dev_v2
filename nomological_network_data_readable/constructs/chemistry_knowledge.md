# Chemistry knowledge
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Chemical knowledge  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** The prevailing framing treats chemistry knowledge as model-embedded or model-encoded understanding of chemistry, centered on chemical principles and structures. Definitions vary only slightly at the boundary, with one adding synthesis rules and another reactions, and the source snippets use the construct both to describe LLMs as lacking in-depth chemistry knowledge and as implicitly encoding substantial chemical knowledge.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not introduce a formal subtype structure for this construct. The recurring facets are chemical principles, structures, and process-oriented knowledge, expressed either as synthesis rules or reactions, and the labels chemistry knowledge and chemical knowledge are used for the same model-level notion.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument and no behavioral indicator are linked to this construct, and no linked constructs are listed. One paper nonetheless treats performance in searching molecular structures with optimized properties as evidence of substantial chemical knowledge, while another invokes lack of in-depth chemistry knowledge as a limitation, but no named benchmark, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge, probe, or analysis is linked to the construct.
