# Linguistic knowledge
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define linguistic knowledge as an internal or latent understanding/proficiency in language structure that supports language processing. They differ mainly in setting and emphasis, such as sign-to-text generation, spoken utterance interpretation, or which linguistic levels are listed, but they name the same core property rather than different phenomena.

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats linguistic knowledge as internal, underlying, or latent knowledge of language that enables understanding, interpretation, generation, or alignment. The papers agree on that core idea, but differ in scope and setting: one frames it around sign language inputs and the generation or alignment of textual outputs, another around understanding and effectively using spoken language, and another around systematic handling of morphological, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic phenomena, sometimes as knowledge embedded within or intrinsically represented in the model.

**Space of facets.** When papers break the construct down, they do so mainly by linguistic level. One account names lexical knowledge, syntax, and semantic coherence, another extends the range to morphology, syntax, semantics, and pragmatics, and the sign-language-oriented usage ties the construct to gloss and text data as a way to inject linguistic knowledge into a pre-trained model. No papers link it to any other named construct.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse: papers link linguistic knowledge to one measurement instrument, BLiMP, and to no behavioral indicators. In the only explicit measure, a paper evaluates model performance on linguistic knowledge through BLiMP, making the construct observable through performance on that instrument, while the definitions describe a broader latent or underlying language proficiency across sign-language alignment and spoken-language or multi-level linguistic competence.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, no papers claim a causal relationship, correlation, or other related link involving linguistic knowledge. The only reported relationship is that it is measured by BLiMP.
