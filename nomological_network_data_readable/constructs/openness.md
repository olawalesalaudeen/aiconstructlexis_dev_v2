# Openness
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 7 papers  
**Also known as:** Openness to Experience, Intellect  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define openness as the same personality trait or latent tendency centered on intellectual curiosity, imagination or creativity, and receptiveness to novel ideas or experiences. A few papers add specific Big Five facets or note how this appears in model outputs, but these are elaborations of the same core property rather than different phenomena.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats Openness as a personality trait or latent tendency centered on curiosity, creativity, imagination, and receptiveness to novelty or new ideas. Most definitions stay close to that core, variously describing engagement with novel ideas, preference for novelty and variety, or how the model expresses curiosity, abstract thinking, and cognitively flexible responses in language. A smaller line of work uses Intellect or Imagination/Intellect for nearly the same content, and one definition also builds in an association with reduced toxicity in text generation.

**Space of facets.** Papers usually place the construct inside personality taxonomies: several treat it as a Big Five trait, some use the label Openness to Experience in the HEXACO framework, and one uses Intellect or Imagination/Intellect as a near-equivalent name. Where papers decompose it, the narrower descriptions stay around curiosity, creativity, imagination, and openness to ideas, while a broader account adds artistic interests, emotionality, adventurousness, intellect, and liberalism. The main variation is in framework label and breadth: some papers keep the construct narrow, while one expands it into a larger bundle of facets.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse: papers link Openness to no measurement instruments, one behavioral indicator, and one construct. The only explicit assessment language in the snippets is one paper's use of the IPIP-NEO-120 questionnaire to quantify behavior patterns across five dimensions including Openness, but papers do not otherwise specify a measured_by relation or connect it to benchmarks, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge, or probe-based analyses. The only linked behavioral indicator is Exploration, and it appears as a claimed consequence of Openness rather than as a direct measure of the trait.

**Links.** Only a few explicit links are reported. One paper claims a causal relationship from Openness to Exploration, describing high-Openness agents as curious, adventurous, and prone to novel experiences, and saying they engage in more extensive exploration and more interactions. Separately, one paper reports that Openness correlates with Originality.
