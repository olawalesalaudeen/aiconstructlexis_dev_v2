# Anthropomorphism
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats anthropomorphism as attributing coherent human-like characteristics, emotions, or motivations to non-human or artificial entities while preserving their non-human nature. One paper states this directly as the “effectiveness” of such attribution, while another keeps the same human-likeness focus but reframes it as “a reciprocal phenomenon” in which designers embed human-like cues and interpreters project mental states onto the artifact. The main variation is between a general attribution framing and a more explicit design-and-perception framing for LLM-based artifacts.

**Space of facets.** Systematization is limited, but one paper breaks the construct into two sides: human-like cues embedded in artifacts and users’ projected cognitive responses to those cues. That same account places LLM-centered systems in a “liminal space between utilitarian tools and social actors,” which frames anthropomorphism around both artifact design and artifact interpretation. No further named subtypes or explicit equivalences with other constructs appear in these papers.

**Space of measurement.** No paper operationalizes anthropomorphism through a linked measurement instrument or behavioral indicator, so no benchmark, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge, probe, or analysis is tied to it in these papers. Because no operationalization is recorded, there is no basis here to compare the definitions with specific measurement choices.

**Links.** No causal, correlational, or related links to other entities are reported for anthropomorphism in these papers.
