# Fluid intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available papers, fluid intelligence is treated as a latent ability for handling novel problems or settings without relying on prior or pre-existing knowledge. The clearest definition adds that this ability involves abstract inductive reasoning and pattern induction from examples, while another paper uses closely matching language and describes it as the capacity to apply learned skills in novel settings. One paper also presents evaluation of fluid intelligence as a way to assess LLM reasoning abilities when memorized knowledge may otherwise be available.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not break the construct into subtypes. Instead, they define it mainly by contrast with reliance on memorization or pre-existing knowledge, and one paper places analogical reasoning alongside it by saying analogy-making is tightly linked with fluid intelligence. The available usage also connects fluid intelligence to reasoning abilities, but does not treat it as interchangeable with them.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow in the available papers: fluid intelligence is linked to one measurement instrument, the ARC task, and to no behavioral indicators. One paper describes ARC as the de facto standard for measuring machine fluid intelligence, so the construct is operationalized through that task and no other linked instrument is named.

**Links.** Outside measurement, one paper reports that analogical reasoning is related to fluid intelligence, describing analogy-making as tightly linked with it. The available papers do not report causal relationships from fluid intelligence to other constructs, or correlational links.
