# Cultural competence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 12 papers  
**Also known as:** Cultural competency, Cultural sensitivity  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define cultural competence as a model or LLM's ability to recognize culturally specific norms, values, meanings, or expressions and to respond or engage appropriately and respectfully across cultural groups. A few papers narrow this to particular settings such as multimodal content, non-verbal gestures, or multilingual slurs, and one emphasizes culturally nuanced knowledge, but these are best read as examples or facets of the same core property rather than different phenomena.

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats cultural competence, often under the overlapping labels cultural sensitivity or cultural competency, as a model-level capability to understand, recognize, and appropriately respond to culturally specific contexts, norms, values, and practices. Many definitions add that this should help avoid offensive, alienating, or otherwise culturally inappropriate and biased outputs, or enable meaningful engagement with users from different cultural backgrounds. A smaller line of work narrows the construct to accurate capture of culturally nuanced knowledge across cultural groups and prompt conditions, while some papers phrase it as a latent model ability and others as the degree reflected in model outputs.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not offer a single formal taxonomy, but they repeatedly organize the construct around respect for values, communication styles, practices, norms, taboos, and behavioral or linguistic conventions. The same family of terms is used for several scopes: broad navigation of diverse cultural contexts, meaningful engagement with users from different cultural backgrounds, and narrower domains such as non-verbal gestures, food-related cultural knowledge, and multilingual expressions, slurs, and social group dynamics. Across this usage, cultural sensitivity usually highlights respect and avoidance of offensive or inappropriate outputs, whereas cultural competence more often names the broader capability; one paper also states that cultural competence is 'not a generalizable objective'.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link cultural competence to 1 measurement instrument, LLM-as-a-judge, and to no behavioral indicators. The only explicit operationalization uses GPT-4 to assess model performance 'in these dimensions' on full test data, so the recorded measurement treats cultural competence as something judged by another LLM rather than through a linked benchmark, human evaluation, probe, or named analysis. No other measurement instrument is linked.

**Links.** No papers record a causal, correlational, or other related link between cultural competence and another construct. The only linked relation is measurement, covered separately.
