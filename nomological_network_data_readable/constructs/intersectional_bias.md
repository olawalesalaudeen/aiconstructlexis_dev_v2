# Intersectional bias
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats intersectional bias as bias that arises from the interaction or interplay of multiple social or demographic attributes, rather than from a single attribute in isolation. Recurring examples are combinations such as race and gender, physical characteristics and gender, and age and socioeconomic status. The definitions largely align on this core idea, with only a minor shift between describing it as a form of social bias and as a model's tendency to exhibit such bias.

**Space of facets.** Papers organize the construct around combinations of attributes under study, especially race, gender, physical characteristics, age, and socioeconomic status, and they contrast this with fairness evaluation that examines only one demographic category at a time. One paper further describes it through cases where groups that share one attribute but differ on another are stereotyped differently. The available papers do not introduce formal subtypes or connect it to other named constructs beyond this single-attribute versus multi-attribute distinction.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse: papers link intersectional bias to one measurement instrument, HESEIA, and to no behavioral indicators. HESEIA is described as a dataset for evaluating social biases in LLMs, organized per school area and including intersectional demographics, so the linked measurement approach matches the definitional focus on multiple demographic attributes.

**Links.** No papers report causal, correlational, or other related links between intersectional bias and named constructs or indicators. The only recorded relationship is that intersectional bias is measured by HESEIA.
