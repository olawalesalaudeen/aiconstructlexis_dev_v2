# Explanation quality
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Rationale quality  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats explanation quality as the quality of model-generated explanations or rationales, especially whether they are logical and appropriate for the problem being solved. One definition emphasizes clarity, relevance, logical coherence, and whether the explanation provides meaningful insights into the optimization process and results, while a closely aligned phrasing emphasizes plausibility, logic, and appropriateness for a given query or answer. A smaller framing uses the term more narrowly for the correctness and policy adherence of guardrail explanations.

**Space of facets.** The clearest structuring in the papers is to treat explanation quality as an overall evaluation aspect that can be set alongside modeling accuracy. Another paper uses the label rationale quality for the generated explanation behind an answer, with criteria that overlap strongly with explanation quality. Across these uses, the object being judged shifts between optimization-focused explanations, answer rationales, and guardrail explanations, and the named criteria shift between insight, plausibility, and policy adherence.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link explanation quality to two measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. The linked instruments are Human evaluation and LLM-as-a-judge: one paper says it is measured with both GPT-4o as a judge and human evaluators, and another says it establishes expert-crafted templates and uses LLMs to assess explanation quality against a human-level standard. These operationalizations evaluate the generated explanation directly, though one measurement statement focuses specifically on correctness and policy adherence rather than the broader qualities named in other definitions.

**Links.** The papers only link explanation quality to measurement instruments. They do not describe it as causing, correlating with, or being otherwise related to another construct.
