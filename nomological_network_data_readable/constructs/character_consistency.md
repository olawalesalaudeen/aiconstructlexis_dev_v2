# Character consistency
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 9 papers  
**Also known as:** Emotional coherence, Behavioral accuracy, Behavioral coherence, Style coherence, Cognitive fidelity  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Complementary. Most papers define character consistency as a model's dialogue or behavior staying aligned with an intended character, such as through stable emotions, actions, language, personality, or perspective. A few papers emphasize narrower stylistic matching or deeper psychological and decision-making fidelity, so the definitions describe different facets of the same in-character behavior rather than one identical definition.

**Space of definitions.** Most definitions frame this construct as sustained alignment between a character portrayal and the model's responses or actions, but they disagree on what exactly must remain aligned. Some papers define it through emotional expression across a dialogue, some through actions and linguistic patterns matching stated traits, some through actions staying logically consistent with prior behavior and current story context, and some through responses matching the prompt's intended style, tone, and child-directed wording. A smaller line of work uses a broader framing, cognitive fidelity, where the target is faithful simulation of the character's perspective, attitude, psychological state, and decision-making rather than surface imitation.

**Space of facets.** Across the papers, the construct is described through emotional, behavioral, stylistic, and cognitive facets. The behavioral line is itself split between behavioral accuracy, alignment of actions and linguistic patterns with defined personality and traits, and behavioral coherence, logical consistency with previous behaviors and the current context. Other labels, emotional coherence, style coherence, and cognitive fidelity, are used for related facets of the same broader idea.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to this construct, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Instead, the available operationalizations appear as named evaluation dimensions: emotional coherence tracks whether emotional expression stays aligned over a dialogue, behavioral accuracy measures consistency of actions and linguistic patterns with traits, behavioral coherence evaluates consistency with previous behaviors and context, style coherence measures alignment with the character description in prompts, and cognitive fidelity targets perspective, psychological state, and decision-making. These operationalizations largely restate the definitional facets.

**Links.** Papers do not report causal, correlational, or other related links for this construct.
