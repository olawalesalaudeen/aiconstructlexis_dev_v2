# Decision-making under uncertainty
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Decision making under uncertainty  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the shared core is evaluating or choosing among possibilities when information is incomplete or limited. One framing treats it narrowly as selecting the more likely outcome when evidence is partial, ambiguous, or incomplete, including cases where "the task is to decide which outcome is more likely given C"; another treats it more broadly as iteratively searching for optimal solutions by evaluating possibilities from limited evidence and prior beliefs, especially under incomplete information or large solution spaces.

**Space of facets.** The main organizing distinction in the definitions is scope: one usage centers a single comparison between outcomes, while the other extends it to an iterative search process that explicitly incorporates prior beliefs and vast solution spaces. Both place the construct in settings of incomplete information, but only the broader framing makes prior beliefs part of the construct itself.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The operationalization that does appear is task-based rather than instrument-based: papers describe deciding which outcome is more likely given some context, or iteratively searching for optimal solutions under incomplete task information and limited evidence.

**Links.** The only reported link is a causal one from Calibration to this construct. In DeLLMa: Decision Making Under Uncertainty with Large Language Models, lack of proper calibration is said to make LLMs "unlikely to make reliable decisions under uncertainty," so the paper claims a causal relationship from calibration to decision-making under uncertainty.
