# Cultural commonsense
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Commonsense cultural reasoning  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** The available papers use two nearby but distinct framings. One paper treats it as shared background knowledge about culturally grounded social expectations that guides appropriate interaction and is "shared within Persian culture but often not intuitive to outsiders" (We Politely Insist: Your LLM Must Learn the Persian Art of Taarof). Another uses the neighboring label "commonsense cultural reasoning" for the capacity to make inferences from everyday, implicit knowledge about cultural norms, practices, and associations, with examples such as attire or food pairing (DRISHTIKON: A Multimodal Multilingual Benchmark for Testing Language Models' Understanding on Indian Culture). The main difference is whether the construct is framed as shared cultural knowledge or as reasoning based on that knowledge.

**Space of facets.** Within the available uses, one paper presents Taarof as a form of cultural commonsense, while another turns "Common Sense Cultural" into a reasoning category for everyday cultural inference. The labels "cultural commonsense" and "commonsense cultural reasoning" are used for closely related ideas, with the first centered on socially grounded expectations and the second on norms, practices, and associations.

**Space of measurement.** The recorded operationalization uses one measurement instrument, DRISHTIKON, and no behavioral indicators. DRISHTIKON measures the construct through a "Common Sense Cultural" category described as requiring everyday cultural inference, and the paper says this category is implemented with semantically rich and reasoning-augmented MCQs, including multi-hop, analogy-based, and common-sense cultural formats. This measurement is stated in inference terms, so it matches the reasoning-oriented definition more directly than the definition centered on shared background knowledge and appropriate interaction.
