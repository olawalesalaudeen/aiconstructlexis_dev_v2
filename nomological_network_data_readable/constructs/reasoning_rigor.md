# Reasoning rigor
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Mathematical rigor, Logical rigor  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All three papers define reasoning rigor as a quality of a model's outputs or solutions that involves strong logical coherence, explicit justification, and verifiable or proof-like inference. The differences are only in emphasis, such as step-by-step derivation, formal proof standards, or clear support for claims, so they are studying the same core property.

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats it as the quality or extent of rigorous reasoning, centered on clear justification, coherent logic, and well-supported conclusions. One paper sharpens this into strict, step-by-step logical inference with verifiable derivations and avoidance of spurious or hallucinated conclusions, another frames it in mathematical proof terms as being logically airtight and adhering to formal proof standards, and another contrasts outputs that give conclusions without thorough justification with ones that construct well-supported arguments with clear reasoning. The main variation is scope and reference standard, from general argumentative support, to multi-step derivation, to formal proof quality.

**Space of facets.** Across the papers, nearby labels, reasoning rigor, mathematical rigor, and logical rigor, are used for overlapping descriptions of justified and coherent reasoning. The most explicit internal breakdown ties it to reasoning granularity and strict step-by-step structure: one framework restricts each step to at most one derivation and treats the resulting granularity as strengthening rigor. Other papers organize the idea around proof-style justification, for example whether a solution makes a claim without justification or meets formal proof standards.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Instead, the papers describe it through qualitative criteria: whether reasoning proceeds step by step with verifiable derivations, whether claims are thoroughly justified, whether logic is coherent, and whether a solution meets formal proof standards. Operationalization therefore appears only through these descriptive criteria, not through a linked instrument.

**Links.** No causal or correlational link is recorded. The only linked construct is Logical reasoning, which one paper treats as related to reasoning rigor in complex, multi-step reasoning tasks, noting difficulty with structured exploration while ensuring logical coherence and rigor.
