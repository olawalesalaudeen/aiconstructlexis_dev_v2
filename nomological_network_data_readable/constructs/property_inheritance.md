# Property inheritance
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Class-knowledge transfer  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define property inheritance as inferring or transferring a property from a general or parent category to its more specific members or subtypes. The differences are only in phrasing and emphasis, such as mentioning novel properties, additional evidence, or explicitly framing it as an LLM ability, rather than changing the core phenomenon.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage describes property inheritance as projecting or transferring a property from a higher-level category, class, or parent type to a lower-level member, subtype, or subordinate entity. One formulation emphasizes projection of 'novel properties' from a general category to specific members (Characterizing the Role of Similarity in the Property Inferences of Language Models), while another asks whether 'a subtype inherits a property from its parent type' and makes the inference explicitly defeasible, meaning it may be weakened or strengthened by additional evidence (Evaluating Defeasible Reasoning in LLMs with DEFREASING). A smaller wording variation recasts this as an LLM's ability for 'class-knowledge transfer'.

**Space of facets.** The papers mainly place the construct in neighboring frames. One paper treats it as a phenomenon within defeasible reasoning and says its dataset focuses on property inheritance as expressed through generics, another uses 'class-knowledge transfer' for querying specific properties across hierarchies of entities. A separate line examines property inheritance behavior in relation to taxonomic relations and categorical similarities.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. No linked constructs are recorded alongside it.
