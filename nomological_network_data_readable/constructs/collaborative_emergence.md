# Collaborative emergence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Emergent ability  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Papers converge only at a broad level: they describe capabilities that arise from collaboration among multiple models or agents, rather than from any one model alone. Beyond that, the definitions diverge. One framing treats it as new, sophisticated capabilities that appear as the number of agents scales, and even appear earlier or at much smaller scales than traditional neural emergence, while another treats it as emergent ability shown when collaborating models give correct answers that individual models cannot provide independently.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not break the construct into named facets or subtypes. The main variation is terminological and contrastive: closely related usage appears under both "collaborative emergence" and "emergent ability," and one framing explicitly positions the former against traditional neural emergence by emphasizing earlier manifestation at smaller scales.

**Space of measurement.** Operationally, the available descriptions are illustrative rather than tied to named instruments. One usage points to cases where a collaborative system provides the correct answer even when each individual model answers incorrectly, but no measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to the construct.
