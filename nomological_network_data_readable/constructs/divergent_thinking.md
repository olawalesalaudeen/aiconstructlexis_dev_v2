# Divergent thinking
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  
**Also known as:** Divergent reasoning  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define divergent thinking as the ability or tendency to produce multiple distinct, often novel ideas, solutions, strategies, or hypotheses from the same starting point or task. The differences are only in emphasis and setting, such as prompt use, human solution space, or expert knowledge, rather than in what phenomenon is being defined.

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats divergent thinking as a capacity or cognitive ability to generate multiple distinct ideas, alternatives, or solution pathways from a single task or starting point. Some definitions add novelty or unconventionality, describing alternatives not previously used in the human solution space and exploration of new environments or constraints, while another ties the capacity to varied in-context examples and few-shot prompts. A smaller line of work uses the label divergent reasoning for generating multiple plausible connections and hypotheses from limited expert knowledge by extrapolating to correlated queried concepts.

**Space of facets.** A recurring framing positions it within creativity, alongside convergent thinking: convergent thinking is described as purposefulness toward a goal, and divergent thinking as adaptability to explore new environments or constraints. In one multi-agent framing, the divergent-thoughts process has two core components, retrieval of relevant tool-usage demos and generation of different few-shot prompts with varied (Q, C, A) tuples, and it proceeds through multiple planning pathways. The terminology is not fully uniform, because one paper uses divergent reasoning for hypothesis and connection generation from limited expert information.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to divergent thinking. Even so, papers describe the DAT as a creativity test in which unrelated words with greater context-embedding distances indicate more divergent thinking, the Alternate Uses Task as a validated psychological test for human divergent thinking, and DSI values as indicating more divergent thinking and greater ideological diversity; one creativity paper also says its NEOGAUGE score mainly considers H-creativity measurement. Another paper makes the construct concrete through multiple planning pathways, demo retrieval, and varied few-shot prompts.

**Links.** Several papers describe creativity as related to divergent thinking, including a framing in which creativity consists of convergent and divergent thinking. One paper claims a causal relationship from divergent thinking to creativity, describing the ability to generate diverse solutions to a problem as a hallmark of creativity. The same paper also claims a causal relationship from divergent thinking to robustness, stating that divergent reasoning enhances robustness.
