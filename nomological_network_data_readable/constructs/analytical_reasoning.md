# Analytical reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** In the available definition, analytical reasoning is framed as the capacity to perform in-depth analysis and draw meaningful conclusions from complex document content, especially financial, technical, or academic material. The source snippets place it among the challenging aspects needed for "full comprehension of long multimodal documents" and among question aspects alongside domain knowledge and visual interpretation (M-LongDoc: A Benchmark For Multimodal Super-Long Document Understanding And A Retrieval-Aware Tuning Framework). No alternative definition is stated by the papers.

**Space of facets.** One paper positions analytical reasoning as one aspect within broader long-document understanding tasks, alongside domain knowledge and visual interpretation. Another paper places it beside other reasoning types, including mathematical reasoning, temporal reasoning, and commonsense reasoning.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to analytical reasoning, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The papers therefore discuss it at the level of definition and task description, without an explicit linked measurement.

**Links.** One paper, TRANSIENTTABLES: Evaluating LLMs' Reasoning on Temporally Evolving Semi-structured Tables, reports analytical reasoning as related to mathematical reasoning, temporal reasoning, and commonsense reasoning. Its evidence quote groups several reasoning types together, including numerical, temporal, and common-sense reasoning.
