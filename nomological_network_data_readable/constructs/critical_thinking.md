# Critical thinking
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 9 papers  
**Also known as:** Reflective judgment, Critical reasoning  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Complementary. Most papers define critical thinking as a model's latent capacity for independent, reasoned evaluation, such as analyzing information, questioning assumptions, and judging correctness. A few papers narrow this to specific manifestations like weighing conflicting viewpoints, critiquing deficient solutions, or refusing flawed instructions, but these are compatible facets of the same broad phenomenon rather than different constructs.

**Space of definitions.** Most commonly, papers treat critical thinking as a latent capacity for objective analysis and evaluation that supports reasoned judgment, often adding questioning assumptions, detecting inconsistencies, or independently weighing conflicting viewpoints. A smaller line of work narrows the construct to cases where a model resists incorrect user influence or flawed instructions, including refusing to choose among invalid options, while another task-specific framing casts it as reviewing a proposed solution for deficiencies, overlooked constraints, and constructive critique. Across these definitions, the main agreement is that it is an evaluative reasoning ability, and the main variation is whether it is defined broadly or through specific settings such as debate, solution review, or invalid multiple-choice prompts.

**Space of facets.** Papers place it in slightly different conceptual neighborhoods. One paper presents critical thinking as part of logical reasoning abilities, while another uses the nearby labels reflective judgment and critical reasoning: reflective judgment is the capacity to question or disregard flawed instructions, and critical reasoning is the underlying capacity that analyzes information and detects inconsistencies. Another paper situates it within an iterative design-and-review pattern, where solution designing alternates with reviewing and the review step generates comments about remaining deficiencies.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Instead, papers describe it through concrete behaviors such as identifying correct answers across varying perspectives, producing review comments that point out deficiencies or overlooked constraints, and recognizing when it is better not to follow instructions because the available options are incorrect. These behaviors line up with the prevailing emphasis on evaluation and judgment, but papers do not connect the construct to a named benchmark, human evaluation protocol, judge model, probe, or analysis.

**Links.** No papers here explicitly relate it to other entities through measured_by, causes, correlates, or related links.
