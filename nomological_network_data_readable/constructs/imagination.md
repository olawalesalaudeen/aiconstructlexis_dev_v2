# Imagination
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, imagination is treated as a visual simulation ability, but the papers split on its target. One framing describes it as generating novel visual representations, specifically intermediate subgoal images corresponding to planned sub-instructions, in support of reasoning before acting. Another framing describes it as mentally simulating or visualizing changes in a scene to infer missing perceptual knowledge and support hypothetical reasoning.

**Space of facets.** Rather than a shared subtype scheme, the papers place the same label in two nearby systems. In one usage, imagination sits alongside reasoning and action, with imagined subgoal images offering more intuitive guidance for sub-instructions; in the other, it sits alongside hypothetical reasoning, where imagination is used to fill perceptual knowledge gaps by deducing missing details and approximating reality. The main distinction is between subgoal-image generation for planned sub-instructions and scene-change simulation for missing perceptual knowledge.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse: imagination is linked to no measurement instrument and no behavioral indicator. Instead, the papers make it concrete through task demands, either generating intermediate subgoal images for planned sub-instructions or imagining a changed scene before reasoning. One paper further reports that performance degrades when models must imagine the scene after changes before reasoning, compared with reasoning directly from a provided scene.

**Links.** Among linked constructs, creativity is the only one that appears. One paper claims a causal relationship from imagination to creativity, stating that reasoning tasks containing imagination may influence a corresponding creative task, illustrated with Tangram Speculation and Tangram Segmentation.
