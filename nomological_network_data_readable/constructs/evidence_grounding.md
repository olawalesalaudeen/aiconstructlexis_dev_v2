# Evidence grounding
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Credibility  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed definitions, the construct is framed around whether answers are anchored in relevant evidence from the input, rather than being unsupported or shortcut-based. One usage defines it as the ability to support responses with specific, relevant, and accurate references to observable content such as visual scenes, while another, under the label credibility, defines it as answers based on a genuine understanding of relevant evidence within video content rather than shortcuts or biases. The common ground is reliance on relevant evidence, but the emphasis differs: one definition centers on explicit, accurate referencing in the response, and the other centers on whether the answer comes from genuine understanding rather than shortcuts.

**Space of facets.** The papers use two nearby labels for overlapping material, evidence grounding and credibility. The evidence-grounding label is described through the quality and relevance of the evidence provided, more precise scene descriptions, behavioral insights, and concrete examples that support assertions, while the credibility label is tied to retrieving relevant clues and to answers grounded in genuine understanding of video evidence rather than shortcuts or biases.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse: no measurement instrument is linked to it, and it is linked to 2 behavioral indicators, Information retrieval and Supportiveness judgment. In the papers' own terms, evaluation turns on the quality and relevance of the evidence provided, the ability to reference specific scenes and provide concrete examples, and the retrieval of relevant clues. These descriptions track the definitions' focus on evidence-based answers, while the credibility framing also adds the language of shortcuts or biases.

**Links.** A single paper claims a causal relationship from Information retrieval to Evidence grounding, stating that retrieval-augmented generation can "enhance factual grounding and reduce hallucination." Another paper treats Evidence grounding as related to Supportiveness judgment, placing it alongside claim decomposition and supportiveness judgment in a multi-step process.
