# Traceability
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing framing treats traceability as the degree to which generated outputs can be traced back to supporting sources, and in one formulation also to reasoning steps, so that users can verify content and carry out error analysis. A narrower, task-specific usage applies the same idea to summarization: summaries are traceable when users can inspect the source sentences, often through sentence-level citations, that support each summarized aspect.

**Space of facets.** The main variation in usage is between a general framing, which links outputs back to specific sources or reasoning steps, and a narrower summarization framing, which focuses on sentence-level traceability from summary content to inspectable source sentences. The surrounding discussion places traceability alongside verification, error analysis, interpretability, and accountability: one paper says opaque generation processes hinder traceability and error analysis, while another emphasizes that tracing source evidence lets users assess summary accuracy.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to traceability, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. In the papers' descriptions, the construct is made concrete through whether outputs are directly traceable to original sources and context, or whether summaries are paired with sentence-level citations so users can inspect the source content that supports the generated text.
