# Clinical decision-making
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Clinical decision making  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** The available paper frames clinical decision-making as a broader latent capacity for making diagnostic and treatment decisions from patient information, clinical evidence, and evolving hypotheses. In that usage, it is described as a complex, multi-step process that includes assessing symptoms, retrieving relevant evidence, formulating diagnostic and treatment strategies, selecting the most probable differential diagnoses, and deciding what additional information is needed to reduce uncertainty and guide next diagnostic and therapeutic steps.

**Space of facets.** Within this framing, papers break the construct into sequential activities rather than named subtypes: symptom assessment, evidence retrieval, diagnostic formulation, treatment planning, differential diagnosis selection, and identifying further information needed for the next step. The available evidence does not equate it with other constructs or introduce alternative labels for a different aspect of the concept.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow in the available relationships: papers link clinical decision-making to one measurement instrument, JAMA Clinical Challenge. One paper describes that instrument as a dataset of complex clinical cases that test decision-making skills in realistic clinical scenarios. No behavioral indicators are linked to the construct, and no other instruments are named.

**Links.** The only reported link is that clinical decision-making is measured by JAMA Clinical Challenge, in How Can We Diagnose and Treat Bias in Large Language Models for Clinical Decision-Making?. Papers do not report causal, correlational, or other related links for this construct.
