# Hierarchical reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Hierarchical thinking  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, hierarchical reasoning is described in two closely related ways. One paper treats it as the ability to solve problems with multiple dependent reasoning steps organized in a hierarchy or sequence, while another frames hierarchical thinking as a latent ability to decompose complex reasoning problems into multiple levels of sub-tasks through a divide-and-conquer strategy for organized planning. The shared core is hierarchically organized, multi-step reasoning, but the emphasis differs between solving such problems and decomposing them into subproblems.

**Space of facets.** Papers use both hierarchical reasoning and hierarchical thinking for closely related capacities. The internal structure they emphasize is multi-level organization: dependent reasoning steps, levels of sub-tasks, and a divide-and-conquer process that one paper describes as reducing the length of reasoning chains while maintaining the planning process. Planning is the clearest application context in the hierarchical thinking framing, whereas the other definition is broader and centers on problem solving with hierarchical or sequential dependencies.

**Space of measurement.** It is linked to 0 measurement instruments and 0 behavioral indicators. The papers instead describe it conceptually through task and process features, such as problems with multiple dependent steps, multi-level sub-task decomposition, divide-and-conquer reasoning, and planning settings where reasoning chains are reduced while the planning process is maintained.

**Links.** Its only linked construct is System 2 thinking. One paper claims a causal relationship from System 2 thinking to hierarchical reasoning, and in that setting describes hierarchical reasoning as perceiving visual tasks with top-down granularity.
