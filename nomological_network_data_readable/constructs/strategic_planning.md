# Strategic planning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 10 papers  
**Also known as:** Strategy planning, Multi-turn planning, Strategic thinking, Strategic dialogue planning  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Complementary. Most papers define strategic planning as a latent ability to formulate, select, or refine multi-step actions or strategies over time to achieve a goal, often with attention to future consequences. A few papers narrow this to dialogue management, adversarial settings, or choosing reasoning paths, so they emphasize different aspects and settings of the same planning phenomenon rather than contradicting each other.

**Space of definitions.** Across these papers, the prevailing usage treats strategic planning as a latent, goal-directed capacity for long-horizon, multi-step decision making: navigating a complex space, planning ahead, weighing future consequences, and refining actions from feedback. Many definitions also stress balancing exploration and exploitation, simulating future rewards or outcomes, or reasoning about opponent actions in adversarial settings. Some authors narrow the construct to dialogue, defining it as guiding conversations through sequences of strategies or questions toward consensus, information extraction, or a target, while a smaller framing describes it as choosing among alternative reasoning paths under uncertainty. The main variation is between a broad planning ability across decision spaces and narrower dialogue-specific or reasoning-specific versions.

**Space of facets.** Papers use several near-overlapping names for related capacities, including strategy planning, multi-turn planning, strategic thinking, and strategic dialogue planning. Within the broad framing, recurring facets are lookahead, future consequence simulation, exploration versus exploitation, and feedback-based refinement; some work also contrasts long-term strategic vision with fine-grained tactical maneuvers or places strategic planning alongside resource management, diplomacy, and warfare. In dialogue-focused work, the construct is specialized into adaptive questioning, persuasion, consensus seeking, target-oriented action sequencing, or selecting from a predefined set of dialogue strategies based on conversational state.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. In the papers, the construct is instead instantiated through task setups such as Monte Carlo tree search over prompt space with future reward lookahead and backpropagated feedback, multi-turn interviewing that directs questions over time, adversarial or non-collaborative dialogue strategy selection, and dialogue action planning toward long-horizon targets. These descriptions operationalize it as choosing or sequencing actions over time toward longer-term goals.
