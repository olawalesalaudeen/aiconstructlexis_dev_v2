# Abductive reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 12 papers  
**Also known as:** Abductive inference  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define abductive reasoning as inferring or generating the most likely or plausible explanation or hypothesis from observations, especially when the information is incomplete or ambiguous. One paper adds prior knowledge, case-specific analysis, and problem solving language, but this is still the same core phenomenon rather than a different construct.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the dominant framing describes abductive reasoning as the ability to generate or infer the most likely, plausible explanation or hypothesis from incomplete observations, ambiguous information, or observations that require explanation. Definitions largely agree on this explanation-seeking core, often phrased as starting from incomplete observations and seeking the most likely explanation. A smaller variation presents it as a latent ability or reasoning strategy that uses prior knowledge and case-specific analysis to infer an explanation or solution and simplify complex problems.

**Space of facets.** Papers most commonly place it under logical reasoning, typically alongside deductive and inductive reasoning, and in some formulations also alongside analogical reasoning. Some work also treats abduction as one reasoning strategy within a larger action space, rather than only as a standalone capability. The literature here uses both "abductive reasoning" and "abductive inference" for the same process of forming explanatory hypotheses.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The available operational detail is limited to task descriptions: one benchmark paper includes abductive reasoning among logical reasoning abilities, and one game paper says a live game and its retrospective analysis evaluate abductive reasoning capabilities, including together with multi-hop reasoning. No shared benchmark, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge setup, or probe is explicitly linked to it.

**Links.** The only recorded link is a related relation to logical reasoning. Several papers place abductive reasoning within logical reasoning, describing logical reasoning as including deductive, inductive, and abductive forms, and in one case also analogical reasoning.
