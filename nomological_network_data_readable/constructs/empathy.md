# Empathy
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 23 papers  
**Also known as:** Emotional support quality, Empathic understanding  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define empathy as a model or response understanding another person's feelings and perspective and conveying that understanding through emotionally attuned, supportive dialogue. A few papers stress particular settings or manifestations such as therapeutic trust, emotional resonance, human-like emotion matching, or overall counseling support, but these are a small minority relative to the shared core definition.

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats empathy as understanding another person's feelings or perspective and showing that understanding in emotionally attuned, supportive language. Definitions describe models, therapists, advice, or responses as emotionally resonant, compassionate, reflective of users' feelings, or responsive to emotional states. A smaller line narrows empathy to whether a model's own feelings change in ways that match what humans would feel in a situation, and one theoretical account instead presents empathy as a complex, multidimensional psychological construct, so the main disagreement is whether empathy is a model feeling change, a property of generated responses, or a broader construct expressed through language.

**Space of facets.** When papers systematize the construct, the clearest account treats it as multidimensional rather than unitary. That account identifies Empathic concern and Personal distress as primary targeted facets, and also notes that empathy may be operationalized as a trait, a state, or a social function in conversation. A smaller line distinguishes a broader emotional support quality, defined as overall effectiveness in supportive counseling dialogue, from a narrower empathic understanding, defined as reflecting feelings and aligning with the user's subjective experience.

**Space of measurement.** Empathy is linked to 4 measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. It is measured by Expert evaluation, G-Eval, Human evaluation, and LLM-as-a-judge, so the operationalizations are human ratings or LLM-based judging of generated outputs; in the linked papers these scores appear in multi-criterion schemes alongside qualities such as logical coherence, guidance, fluency, coherence, naturalness, and patient-centricity. These operationalizations align most directly with definitions that cast empathy as conveyed understanding or supportive response quality, rather than with the narrower definition based on a model's feelings changing with the situation.

**Links.** The recorded construct links are all related links, spanning 3 constructs. One paper reports that Empathy is related to Helpfulness in advice, and another links Empathy to Personal distress while linking Empathic concern to Empathy, describing distress and empathic concern as primary facets targeted within the construct.
