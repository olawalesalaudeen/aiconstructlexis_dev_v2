# Defeasible reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Non-monotonic reasoning  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define defeasible reasoning as a non-monotonic reasoning ability where conclusions are provisional and may be revised, weakened, or withdrawn when new evidence appears. One definition adds the setting of incomplete information and plausible inference, but this is an elaboration of the same core property rather than a different phenomenon.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats defeasible reasoning as non-monotonic reasoning: an inference or prior conclusion can be revised when new evidence appears. Most formulations share this update-or-retract framing, using language such as inferences being altered in light of new evidence or previously accepted statements being revoked when contradictions emerge. Some authors describe it more specifically as making plausible inferences from incomplete information, where later information can strengthen, weaken, or defeat a conclusion.

**Space of facets.** Papers consistently place the construct under non-monotonic reasoning, and one paper uses the label non-monotonic reasoning for the capability to revise prior conclusions in light of new evidence. Another naming variation is defeasible inference. The main internal breakdown stated in the definitions is by how new information affects a conclusion, namely strengthening, weakening, or defeating it, and one paper contrasts this kind of reasoning with traditional NLI as a different type of natural language reasoning.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link it to no measurement instruments, one behavioral indicator, and no constructs. The only linked behavioral indicator is Backtracking, and that link is recorded as related rather than measured_by. This leaves the recorded operationalization narrow, with the available behavior matching the definitions' emphasis on revising assumptions or conclusions when new evidence appears.

**Links.** The only recorded link is that Backtracking is related to defeasible reasoning. One paper describes some logical puzzles as requiring non-monotonic reasoning strategies that involve backtracking and revising assumptions. Papers do not report causal or correlational links for this construct.
