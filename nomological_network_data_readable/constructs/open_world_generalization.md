# Open-world generalization
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Open-set generalization  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing framing is an ability to recognize, process, or predict categories, concepts, or entities that were not present during training. One paper casts this in video terms, as correctly processing unseen object categories or concepts and, more concretely, detecting, segmenting, tracking, and richly captioning never-before-seen objects. Another paper, using the nearby label open-set generalization, treats it as a latent ability to recognize and process unseen multimodal entities and explicitly ties it to broad multimodal knowledge. The shared core is therefore unseen-at-training generalization, with variation mainly in scope, video objects versus multimodal entities, and in whether broad multimodal knowledge is foregrounded.

**Space of facets.** The papers organize the construct mainly through task capabilities and nearby labels rather than a formal taxonomy. In the video-oriented usage, it covers detecting, segmenting, tracking, and describing previously seen or unseen objects; in the multimodal usage, it appears under the related names open-set generalization and open-set abilities, and is discussed together with open-world visual perception and the knowledge capacity of MLLMs.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators are linked to this construct. Instead, the papers make it concrete through task descriptions: detecting, segmenting, tracking, and richly captioning unseen objects in video, or recognizing and processing unseen multimodal entities.

**Links.** No papers report causal, correlational, or related links involving this construct.
