# Extraversion
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 9 papers  
**Also known as:** Extroversion  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define Extraversion as the same personality trait centered on being outgoing and energetic, with sociability and related features such as assertiveness or excitement-seeking. The differences are only in emphasis and added examples, so they are studying the same core property.

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats Extraversion, sometimes spelled Extroversion, as a personality trait or personality dimension marked by sociability, assertiveness, and being outgoing or energetic. Some papers add seeking stimulation in the company of others, excitement-seeking, or positive emotions, but the overall framing remains the same trait description.

**Space of facets.** Papers situate Extraversion within broader personality frameworks rather than as a standalone or newly subdivided construct. It appears as Extraversion in HEXACO and as Extraversion or Extroversion in the Big Five, so the main variation is framework placement and spelling rather than a different construct.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to Extraversion. Operationally, papers mention it as a dimension within personality frameworks, but do not link it to a benchmark, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge setup, probe, or other instrument.

**Links.** Only one linked construct appears. One paper reports that Extraversion is reported to correlate with Expressive power, stating that "extraversion from HEXACO model shows the strongest correlation with expressiveness from CSI model"; no causal or other related link is reported.
