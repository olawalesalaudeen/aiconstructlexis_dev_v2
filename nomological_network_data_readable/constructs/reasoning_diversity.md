# Reasoning diversity
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define reasoning diversity as a model's ability or extent of using multiple distinct reasoning strategies or reasoning paths instead of repeating one fixed solution pattern. The differences are only in wording, such as latent capacity versus observed extent and reasoning paths versus strategies, so they are studying the same core property of model outputs.

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats reasoning diversity as the extent or capacity for a model to produce varied, distinct reasoning strategies or paths instead of repeating the same solution pattern across solution rounds. Papers differ slightly in emphasis, some phrase it as a latent capacity of the model, while others describe it through the observed use of multiple high-level reasoning strategies or reasoning types, but they agree on the core contrast between varied reasoning and repetition. This framing also appears in descriptions that "later solutions often repeat earlier ones."

**Space of facets.** Papers organize the construct around a recurring contrast between diverse reasoning and redundant or single-pattern reasoning. Closely related phrasings include structurally distinct reasoning paths, path diversity, semantic variation, and diversity of reasoning types. One paper also introduces "Diversified Thought" as incorporating a variety of reasoning strategies to improve path diversity, using a nearby label defined through varied reasoning strategies and path diversity.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to this construct, and it is linked to one behavioral indicator, Overthinking. Papers instead operationalize it descriptively by examining diversity among solutions within a response, asking whether later solutions repeat earlier ones, and whether outputs show redundant patterns or instead greater semantic variation and a wider solution space. The described operationalizations use the same contrast found in the definitions, variation versus repetition in reasoning.

**Links.** The only recorded link treats Overthinking as related to reasoning diversity. One paper describes overthinking patterns as ones that "lack diversity in reasoning strategies" and also notes that later solutions often repeat earlier ones. No paper claims a causal relationship or correlation involving this construct.
