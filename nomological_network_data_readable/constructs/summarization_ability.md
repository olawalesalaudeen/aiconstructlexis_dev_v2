# Summarization ability
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  
**Also known as:** Summarization, Summarization capability  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define summarization ability as the ability of a model or agent to distill longer source material into a concise summary that captures the important content. The differences are mainly the input setting and emphasis, such as video, conversation history, narrative depth, or summary quality, but they all study the same core property of producing summaries from longer inputs.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage is a latent or cognitive ability to distill longer inputs into concise summaries while preserving what is most salient or relevant. Most definitions emphasize identifying, organizing, and synthesizing salient content from extended material, whether long video, long-form narrative, conversation or execution history, or longer documents; some add quality criteria such as "high-quality, concise, and relevant" summaries, and some specify preserving events, interactions, sequences, emotional arcs, and thematic depth. A smaller difference is whether authors describe it as a "latent ability" of a model or as a "cognitive ability," but the shared focus remains summarization of long inputs.

**Space of facets.** The papers use the same label across several source materials rather than breaking it into named subtypes. In HourVideo: 1-Hour Video-Language Understanding, summarization appears as one task in a broader suite alongside perception, visual reasoning, and navigation; in InfiniBench: A Benchmark for Large Multi-Modal Models in Long-Form Movies and TV Shows, it is framed as more than "just factual recall," with summaries expected to capture events, emotional arcs, and thematic depth. Other papers use the term for reorganizing conversation or execution history into feedback for the user, or for generating concise summaries from longer documents.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to this construct, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The available descriptions instead center on tasks such as producing concise summaries from long videos or narratives, reorganizing conversation or execution history into distilled feedback to the user, and generating high-quality, relevant summaries from longer documents. These descriptions repeat the same elements used in the definitions: distilling longer material into concise summaries.

**Links.** No causal, correlational, or related links are reported for this construct.
