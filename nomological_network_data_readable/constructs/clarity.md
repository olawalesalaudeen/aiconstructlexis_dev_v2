# Clarity
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 17 papers  
**Also known as:** Writing clarity, Manner  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define clarity as the understandability of a generated text or response: it is clearly expressed, well structured, and free from ambiguity or confusion. A few papers add specific facets such as simplicity, layout, fluency, or appropriate complexity, but they still describe the same core property of communicative outputs rather than a different phenomenon.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats clarity as a property of generated content: questions, responses, summaries, arguments, comments, and hypotheses are clear when they are understandable, clearly formulated, well-structured, and not ambiguous. Most papers stay close to this output-level framing, while some add scope-specific emphases such as coherence and fluency, plain and simple language, or language and layout clarity. A smaller line of work broadens the construct into a model-level ability under the label Manner, defined as expressing contributions clearly and with appropriate complexity for the task.

**Space of facets.** Recurring facets of clarity include unambiguity, understandable language, structure, coherence or fluency, and, in one formulation, layout. In scope-specific uses, papers describe clarity as avoiding overwhelming users in moderation comments, making argument structure and language transparent, or stating hypotheses clearly enough for human or model use. One definition also uses the label writing clarity for the same latent text quality, while a survey uses Manner for a broader principle of clear expression with appropriate complexity.

**Space of measurement.** Clarity is linked to 1 measurement instrument and no behavioral indicators. The recorded instrument is LLM-as-a-judge: one paper reports LLM-based grading of samples before and after self-correction in terms of clarity, together with grammaticality, factuality, writing style, and creativity. Papers also operationalize clarity as a scored evaluation dimension, including five-point ratings for responses and summaries, survey judgments of hypotheses, and direct checks of whether a generated question is clear or ambiguous; these operationalizations target the same themes of understandability, structure, and lack of ambiguity that dominate the definitions.

**Links.** The only recorded relationship is that one paper measures clarity with LLM-as-a-judge. No papers report causal relationships, correlations, or related links between clarity and other constructs.
