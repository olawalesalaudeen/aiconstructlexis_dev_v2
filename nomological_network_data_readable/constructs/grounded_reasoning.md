# Grounded reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, grounded reasoning is framed as reasoning or responding that is based on some form of first-hand or actual evidence rather than on abstract knowledge alone or plausible but unsupported content. In Grounded Answers for Multi-agent Decision-making Problem through Generative World Model, the emphasis is on simulated, first-hand experience within an environment's dynamics, with trial and error described as necessary to ground reasoning on complicated problems. In Re-Align: Aligning Vision Language Models via Retrieval-Augmented Direct Preference Optimization, the construct is defined as a latent capacity to base responses on actual visual evidence, with failures described as fabricated details about objects, attributes, and logical relationships in an input image. The main variation is the source of grounding, simulated environmental experience in one usage and visual evidence in the other.

**Space of facets.** The papers organize the construct through contrasts. One usage sets it against abstract knowledge alone and ties it to trial and error within an environment, while another contrasts it with plausible but unsupported content and with inaccurate or fabricated visual details, and also refers to plausible alternative grounding for an instruction. Across these uses, the salient distinction is what the reasoning or response is grounded in.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to grounded reasoning, so the construct is used without a named operationalization.

**Links.** No paper claims a causal relationship, correlation, or other related link involving grounded reasoning.
