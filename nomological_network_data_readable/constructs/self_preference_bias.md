# Self-preference bias
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 7 papers  
**Also known as:** Self-preference, Model bias, Self-enhancement bias  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Complementary. Most papers define self-preference bias as an LLM judge giving undue preference to its own responses over other models' responses, independent of true quality. A few broaden this to favoritism toward same-family models or to self-referential behavior that generates or evaluates content aligned with the model's own strengths, so the definitions stay compatible as variations on self-advantaging evaluation behavior but are not identical in scope.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the dominant framing treats self-preference bias as a latent tendency in LLM judging: a model favors its own responses, or assigns them higher scores, when it serves as judge, independent of the actual quality of those responses. Closely related definitions use the names self-preference or self-enhancement bias for the same pattern of favoring a model's own outputs over other models' outputs. A smaller line of work broadens the construct to favoritism toward outputs from the same family or similar training patterns, and one paper uses model bias for unfair advantage in self-referential evaluation when content aligns with the model's implicit strengths.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not present a stable subtype scheme, but they do group several labels around overlapping uses. Self-preference bias, self-preference, and self-enhancement bias all denote favoritism toward a model's own answers, while model bias is defined in one paper as generated or evaluated content aligning with the model's implicit strengths and giving it an unfair advantage. One paper also treats it as related to evaluation bias, placing it within a broader evaluation-bias decomposition.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link the construct to 1 measurement instrument, LLM-as-a-judge, and to no behavioral indicators. In the source descriptions, operationalization is mainly through judge settings in which an LLM evaluates responses, most explicitly with a DBG score defined as the difference between the scores a judge assigns to its own responses and the corresponding gold judgments; other snippets describe the pattern as models rating their own outputs more favorably. These operationalizations center on judging behavior, whereas the model-bias formulation also includes generated or evaluated content aligning with the model's implicit strengths.

**Links.** Relationship evidence is limited. One causal-perspective paper claims a causal relationship from LLM-as-a-judge to self-preference bias, stating that agents-as-an-evaluator exhibit model bias, and the same paper reports that self-preference bias is related to evaluation bias through an evaluation-bias decomposition.

> The latent tendency of a large language model to assign higher scores to its own generated responses when serving as a judge, independent of the actual quality of those responses.

**[Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/fdca08d371e4b6c031397909e20043bd-Paper-Conference.pdf) (as "Self-enhancement bias")**
