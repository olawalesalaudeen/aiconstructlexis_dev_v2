# Pedagogical ability
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Pedagogical alignment  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing framing treats this construct as a tutoring-oriented model property aimed at supporting student learning rather than giving answers directly. One paper presents it as a latent capacity to guide learning through strategies such as scaffolding, questioning, and error diagnosis, while a closely related formulation describes an assistant-to-tutor shift and defines pedagogical alignment as the degree to which a model behaves like an effective tutor by helping students construct solutions independently. The shared core is learning support over answer delivery, with the main variation being whether papers phrase it as a latent capacity or as alignment in observed tutoring behavior.

**Space of facets.** Papers organize the construct around teaching skills rather than simple answer provision. The facets named in definitions and snippets include subject expertise, diagnosing and correcting student mistakes, scaffolding, questioning, error diagnosis, and the use of sound pedagogical techniques. The terminology is not fully uniform: one line uses pedagogical ability or pedagogical capabilities of the tutor, while another uses pedagogical alignment for the assistant-to-tutor shift, but both point to effective tutoring behavior.

**Space of measurement.** Papers do not link this construct to any measurement instruments or behavioral indicators. In these papers, it is described through tutoring behaviors such as structured scaffolding and mistake diagnosis, but no measurement procedure is explicitly linked to the construct.

**Links.** No causal, correlational, or related links to other constructs are reported.
