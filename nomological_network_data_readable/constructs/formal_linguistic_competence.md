# Formal linguistic competence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** The only explicit definition treats formal linguistic competence as an ability to generate fluent and grammatically correct text from statistical patterns in training data. In that framing, it is described as arising from statistical learning on large text corpora and as reflecting surface-level 'data' memory rather than deep semantic understanding; no alternative definition is stated.

**Space of facets.** The available definition does not break the construct into subtypes or facets. Instead, it is positioned through a contrast: fluent, grammatical text generation is placed on the side of statistical learning and surface-level memory, rather than on the side of deep semantic understanding.

**Space of measurement.** Formal linguistic competence is linked to 2 measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. The operationalization is benchmark-based: one paper assesses it with BLiMP and SyntaxGym, explicitly describing them as "two benchmarks" used to assess the construct.

**Links.** One paper claims a causal relationship from formal linguistic competence to brain alignment, stating that it "actively drives brain alignment during the early phases of training."
