# Semantic richness
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** The available definition treats semantic richness as a property of generated captions: the degree to which they capture complex, diverse, and contextually grounded meanings rather than simple or template-like descriptions. In Learning to See through Sound: From VggCaps to Multi2Cap for Richer Automated Audio Captioning, this usage is accompanied by descriptions of longer captions, richer and more diverse vocabulary, higher readability complexity, and content descriptions framed as more complex.

**Space of facets.** The construct is not elaborated into named subtypes here. Instead, the available description situates semantic richness next to diversity, vocabulary richness, readability complexity, and more complex content description, rather than distinguishing explicit facets or contrasting definitions.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instruments or behavioral indicators are linked to semantic richness. The available operationalization is descriptive: authors discuss semantically richer captions through longer average caption length, richer and more diverse vocabulary, higher readability complexity, and more complex content descriptions, rather than through a named instrument.

**Links.** A separate paper claims a causal relationship from semantic richness to audio understanding. In ALMTokenizer: A Low-bitrate and Semantic-rich Audio Codec Tokenizer for Audio Language Modeling, this appears in a report that, within the same audio language model framework, the tokenizer "outperforms previous tokenizers in audio understanding and generation tasks."
