# Emotional intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define emotional intelligence as an agent or model's ability to perceive, understand or interpret, and appropriately respond to emotions in interaction. A few definitions broaden this to include managing emotions, social situations, or emotions of oneself and others, but these are scope and emphasis differences around the same core capability rather than different phenomena.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats emotional intelligence as a capability or latent ability of a system, agent, or model to perceive, understand or interpret, and respond appropriately to emotions. Most definitions place this in interaction, especially conversation, while some broaden it to social interactions or social situations, and one paper focuses the application on interpersonal conflict resolution. A smaller variation adds managing emotions, and another defines it as responding to the emotions of oneself and others.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not offer a shared subtype taxonomy. The only explicit part-whole statement treats emotional fidelity as one aspect of emotional intelligence; otherwise, authors mostly locate the construct in task settings such as emotional support dialogue, emotion recognition in conversations, and interpersonal conflict resolution. Beyond emotional fidelity, the papers do not name recurring subcomponents.

**Space of measurement.** Emotional intelligence is linked to 2 measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. The named instruments are Chatbot Arena and EQ-Bench, both described as benchmarks, and one paper also studies the construct by having a council of recent LLMs rank open-ended responses to interpersonal conflicts. Together, these operationalizations use benchmark performance, including an EI-focused subset of Chatbot Arena, and ranking of open-ended responses to interpersonal conflicts, rather than direct behavioral indicators.

**Links.** The only linked construct outside measurement is Creativity. One paper claims a causal relationship from emotional intelligence to creativity, stating that some reasoning tasks needing emotional intelligence influence relative creation tasks. No papers report correlational or broader related links.
