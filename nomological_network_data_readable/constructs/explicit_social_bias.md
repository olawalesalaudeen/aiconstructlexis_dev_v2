# Explicit social bias
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Explicit bias  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing framing treats explicit social bias as bias that is consciously or intentionally expressed and accessible to direct report or control. One definition ties it specifically to models making overt judgments about demographic attributes, while another states it more broadly as bias that is not automatic, relatively controllable, and captured with direct measures. The shared core is directness, reportability, and non-automatic expression, while the main difference is whether the construct is defined narrowly around overt demographic judgments by models or more broadly as a kind of explicit association or belief.

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly organize this construct in contrast to implicit bias. On the explicit side, authors describe fairness-related questions and tasks where models make direct judgments, and one paper refers to explicit associations as individual beliefs; on the contrasting side, the same work distinguishes implicit tasks such as image description and form completion. Beyond this explicit versus implicit split, the papers do not introduce finer subtypes.

**Space of measurement.** Operationally, the construct is linked to one measurement instrument, VISBIAS, and to no behavioral indicators. VISBIAS is described as detecting explicit bias through fairness-related direct judgment tasks, specifically multiple-choice and yes-no questions, while its implicit side uses other task formats. This operationalization follows the definitions closely by using direct measures for bias that is directly expressed or directly reportable.

**Links.** Papers do not report causal, correlational, or generic related links between this construct and other constructs. The only reported relation is measurement: explicit social bias is measured by VISBIAS.
