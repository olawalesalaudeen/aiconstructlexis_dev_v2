# Multimodal perception
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 8 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define multimodal perception as a latent ability to process, integrate, and understand information from multiple modalities, especially visual and textual inputs, often in an environment or unified representation. One paper narrows this to charts and names specific visual structures like axes and legends, but it is still the same core ability applied in a particular setting rather than a different phenomenon.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats multimodal perception as a latent ability to perceive, process, and integrate information from multiple modalities, usually visual and textual inputs, so that an agent or model can understand surroundings, status, objects, or interleaved image-text content. Some descriptions attach the ability to an agent and others to a model, but both use the same cross-modal comprehension framing. The main variation is scope, with broad environment or game-centered descriptions alongside a narrower chart-focused usage that emphasizes extracting chart elements and recognizing colors, structures, axes, legends, and spatial relationships.

**Space of facets.** Papers usually present it as a broad ability, not as a family of named subconstructs. One evaluation framing breaks perception into general, knowledge, OCR, and vision-centric task types, and some papers describe it as foundational for later interaction or as a function that provides detailed descriptions of agent status and environmental features. It is also discussed alongside cognition or local reasoning rather than equated with them, and no linked constructs are recorded.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is benchmark-based: papers link multimodal perception to 14 measurement instruments and 1 behavioral indicator. The most frequently linked instruments are MME, AI2D, ChartQA, and DocVQA, while chart question answering is the only linked behavioral indicator; many other benchmarks are also used, and one paper groups them under general, knowledge, OCR, and vision-centric perception tasks. These operationalizations follow the same perception framing used in the definitions, and chart question answering directly targets the chart-specific version of the construct.

**Links.** No paper reports causal, correlational, or generic related-to links for this construct. The recorded relationships only say that it is measured by benchmark instruments and by chart question answering.
