# Figurative language understanding
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Figurative competence  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the construct is used in two distinct ways rather than one settled definition. One paper treats it as a latent interpretive ability: understanding utterances whose intended meaning diverges from the literal meaning, including irony or hyperbole, by reasoning about rhetorical strategy, and it describes this as enabling "human-compatible interpretations of non-literal utterances." Another paper uses the nearby label figurative competence for a broader, context-sensitive ability to produce and interpret figurative expressions with pragmatic appropriateness in communicative settings, and explicitly says this requires more than recognizing figurative markers in isolation.

**Space of facets.** The papers organize the construct around different facets. One framing centers rhetorical strategy, non-literalness, and the communicative goals of speakers and listeners; another reframes it as a usage-level, discourse-level, context-sensitive ability. The naming also varies: one paper speaks of figurative language understanding, while another uses figurative competence for the broader production-and-interpretation framing.

**Space of measurement.** The recorded operationalization is limited. No measurement instrument is linked to the construct, and it is linked to two behavioral indicators, metaphor detection and sarcasm detection; in the recorded relationships, both appear as related tasks rather than as instruments that measure the construct. The only concrete operational connections recorded are therefore to task labels, not to benchmarks or other named instruments.

**Links.** The recorded links are all related links. One paper relates figurative language understanding to sarcasm detection and to metaphor detection, stating that its analyses and experiments focus on "two types of figurative language (sarcasm and metaphors)," which places both tasks alongside this construct as related behavioral indicators.
