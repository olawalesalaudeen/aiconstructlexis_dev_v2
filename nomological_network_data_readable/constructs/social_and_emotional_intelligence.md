# Social and emotional intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Social/emotional intelligence, Socio-emotional skills  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define social and emotional intelligence as a capability to understand social interactions and emotional context, including others' intentions, feelings, and the nuances of interaction. The differences are mainly in wording and scope, with one paper phrasing it as question answering and another adding management of social and emotional aspects, but they still target the same core ability.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats this as an ability, or latent ability, to understand social and emotional information in interaction, especially intentions, feelings, actions, and the nuances of social context. Most definitions stay at that general level, though one paper uses a narrower, task-tied formulation: the ability to answer questions about social interactions in a way that reflects such understanding. A smaller variation also makes management part of the construct, describing socio-emotional skills as understanding and managing the social and emotional aspects of interaction.

**Space of facets.** The papers use closely related labels, including social and emotional intelligence, social/emotional intelligence, and socio-emotional skills. Named facets include introspection, predicting the feelings of others, and understanding other people's intentions, actions, feelings, and social context; one paper also places theory of mind alongside socio-emotional skills rather than treating it as the same term. Another paper treats it as a domain that language models do not capture well, contrasting it with physical commonsense.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link it to 2 measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. The more recurrent operationalization is SIQA, described as a dataset used to study social and emotional intelligence and as measuring it through questions about social interactions; a smaller line of work also measures it with EQ-Bench. The only described task format is question answering about social interactions, while the broader definitions also mention management, introspection, and predicting others' feelings.
