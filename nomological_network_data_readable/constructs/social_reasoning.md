# Social reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 12 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage frames social reasoning around using social cues and context to reason about human behavior. The most common formulation describes the ability to interpret human events, norms, cues, and contexts, especially in visual narratives, and accompanying descriptions stress human norms, conflicting feelings, and subtle cues tied to cultural backgrounds. A smaller framing treats it as a latent model ability to draw on identity cues and interaction dynamics to anticipate and respond to group-level social behaviors, such as leader selection and peer evaluation. The main disagreement is about scope and setting: interpretation of socially loaded narratives versus anticipation of collective behavior.

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly organize the construct through nearby social cognition concepts rather than a detailed subtype scheme. One paper presents this form of reasoning as a core competency of social intelligence, while another connects it to competencies relevant to theory of mind, specifically interpreting the goals and beliefs of characters.

**Space of measurement.** Papers operationalize social reasoning with 3 linked measurement instruments and no linked behavioral indicators. The recurring instruments are SIQA, BBQ, and MuSR: SIQA is used specifically for social reasoning, BBQ is used for social reasoning tasks through multiple-choice QA about biases against protected groups, and MuSR is tied to domains involving social situations, theory-of-mind, and other commonsense reasoning. These operationalizations center on named tasks and datasets about social situations, multiple-choice QA, and bias evaluation, which do not directly reproduce the visual-narrative or group-level-behavior language used in the definitions.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, papers only report related links. Social reasoning is related to social intelligence, with one paper describing this form of reasoning as a core competency of social intelligence, and it is related to theory of mind through tasks that probe the goals and beliefs of characters. No causal or correlational links are reported.
