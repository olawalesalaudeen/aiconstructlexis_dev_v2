# Domain-specific reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the shared framing is a latent ability to apply knowledge from a particular domain to interpret dialogue in context. A technical-dialogue usage casts it as interpreting utterances, diagnosing issues, and inferring likely misunderstandings, and describes it as especially needed for subtler, more context-dependent cases of misalignment. Another usage applies the same construct to emotional support dialogue, where the domain knowledge comes from psychological theories and expert-like reasoning about emotional states, causal events, and user intentions.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not provide a formal subtype taxonomy for this construct. Instead, they position it through nearby ideas: one paper places it alongside pragmatic reasoning when discussing subtle misalignment, while another associates it with psychological theory, expert cooperation, and dialogue analysis from multiple perspectives for extracting user-related information. The main variation across definitions is the target domain, technical troubleshooting in one usage and emotional support in another, while both treat it as a latent ability rather than an observable behavior.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to it, and no linked construct is used to operationalize it. In these papers it remains a conceptual description rather than an explicit benchmark, human evaluation scheme, LLM-as-a-judge setup, or probe or analysis.

**Links.** No causal, correlational, or related links are reported for this construct.
