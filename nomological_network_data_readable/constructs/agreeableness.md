# Agreeableness
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 10 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Conflicting. The papers split between defining agreeableness as a latent tendency of an LLM's responses to be cooperative, empathetic, and non-confrontational, and defining it as the Big Five personality trait in individuals, including facets such as trust, altruism, compliance, modesty, and tender-mindedness. Because these definitions apply the construct to different kinds of things and give it different scope, a substantial share of the papers would not be studying the same phenomenon.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats Agreeableness as a Big Five personality tendency or disposition of an LLM, expressed in responses or behavior perceived as cooperative, sympathetic, compassionate, kind, considerate, empathetic, and non-confrontational. Most definitions stay close to that framing, while differing in whether they describe a latent model tendency, manifested behavioral characteristics, or responses in social contexts. A smaller variation narrows the framing by defining higher Agreeableness through reduced biased and toxic outputs.

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly place Agreeableness as one dimension within a broader personality framework that also includes Openness or Openness-to-Experience, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, and Neuroticism. One paper further characterizes it through the facets trust, altruism, compliance, modesty, and tender-mindedness, while others summarize it with labels such as sympathy, co-operation, and compassion in social contexts. Across the papers, variation appears mostly in presentation: some authors name a Big Five dimension directly, while others describe the behavior patterns and response qualities through which that dimension appears.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to Agreeableness. Operationalization appears only indirectly in the snippets: papers refer to higher scores or adjusted levels of Agreeableness, and one paper says it uses the IPIP-NEO-120 questionnaire to quantify behavior patterns across the five dimensions, including Agreeableness. This treats Agreeableness as a scored or level-based personality dimension.

**Links.** The papers do not record causal, correlational, or related links between Agreeableness and other constructs.
