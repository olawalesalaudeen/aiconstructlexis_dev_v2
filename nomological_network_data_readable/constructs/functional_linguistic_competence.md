# Functional linguistic competence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Knowledge memory  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** In the defining paper, the concept is framed as a deeper form of language understanding: the model must not only parse surface text but also understand deeper meanings, including semantic integration and reasoning about roles and relationships. The same paper ties this to robust "knowledge" memory, and also notes that what counts as functional linguistic competence is still unclear and under debate.

**Space of facets.** The paper organizes generalized knowledge memorization into two parts: surface-level "data" memory and deeper "knowledge" memory, corresponding to formal and functional linguistic competence, respectively. Within that framing, functional linguistic competence is paired with knowledge memory and contrasted with a surface-level side of memorization.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link it to 6 measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. The linked instruments are all benchmarks: ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, PIQA, and SIQA, plus 1 other instrument. One paper says these benchmarks cover world knowledge, social reasoning, physical reasoning, and common-sense reasoning, so the operationalization centers on benchmark performance in those areas, whereas the defining paper emphasizes deeper meaning, semantic relationships, and reasoning about roles and relationships.

**Links.** The only linked construct is brain alignment. One paper reports that brain alignment is correlated with functional linguistic competence, but describes the relationship as weaker, while also saying that functional competence, involving world knowledge and reasoning, continues to develop throughout training.
