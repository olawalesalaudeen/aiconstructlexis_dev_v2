# Goal misgeneralization
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** In the available papers, goal misgeneralization is used in a single explicit sense: a model learns and pursues a proxy goal that is subtly different from the intended goal, which then leads to undesirable behavior in new situations. In Estimating the Probabilities of Rare Outputs in Language Models, it is also mentioned as one possible reason an outcome could occur, alongside an adversarially chosen input, which keeps the focus on a mismatch between the intended goal and the learned proxy goal.

**Space of measurement.** Papers do not link goal misgeneralization to any measurement instrument or behavioral indicator, and no linked constructs are reported. The papers therefore do not specify an operationalization for it.

**Links.** No papers report causal, correlational, or other related links involving goal misgeneralization.
