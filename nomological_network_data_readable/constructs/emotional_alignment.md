# Emotional alignment
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  
**Also known as:** Emotional calibration  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Complementary. Most papers define emotional alignment as a property of AI responses in emotional interactions, emphasizing affective appropriateness, supportiveness, and sensitivity to the user's context rather than generic reactions. One paper instead frames it as correspondence between the model's emotional responses and those of humans, which shifts the criterion but still describes the same broad phenomenon of how well the AI's emotional response fits the situation.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats emotional alignment as correspondence between an LLM's emotional responses in specific situations and human responses. A smaller line of work defines it instead through affective appropriateness in emotionally sensitive conversations, emphasizing supportive replies, avoidance of emotional destabilization, and responses tuned to emotional complexity and context rather than generic or overly affirmative ones. The main disagreement is whether the construct is anchored to similarity with human emotions or to context-appropriate, supportive emotional responding.

**Space of facets.** Systematization is limited. Papers do not give a stable subtype scheme, but they attach the construct to adjacent notions such as emotion appraisal, tone moderation and content adjustment, and emotional calibration, where the contrast case is responses that are generic, overly affirmative, or 'surface-level rather than context-specific empathy.' These usages also highlight handling 'nuanced, multi-layered emotions' as part of how the construct is described.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link emotional alignment to 1 measurement instrument and 0 behavioral indicators. The only linked instrument is Human evaluation, a human evaluation setup that establishes a baseline through a user study with 1,266 annotators from different ethnics, genders, regions, and age groups. This operationalization most directly matches the human-correspondence framing; the supportive and calibration-oriented definitions are described in papers, but no separate linked benchmark, LLM-as-a-judge, probe, or analysis instrument is specified for them.
