# Spatial visualization
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** The papers treat spatial visualization as an ability centered on mentally manipulating and transforming 2D or 3D stimuli and complex spatial configurations. The wording is consistent across the available definition and snippet, including the phrase "the ability to mentally manipulate 2D or 3D stimuli" in Does Spatial Cognition Emerge in Frontier Models, and no alternative framing is introduced.

**Space of facets.** Systematization is limited, but one paper places spatial visualization among the skills evaluated in small-scale tasks. In that usage, it appears alongside spatial orientation, spatial perception, selective spatial attention, and visuospatial working memory, in tasks that require understanding space at the level of objects or object arrangements.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse. No measurement instrument and no behavioral indicator are linked to spatial visualization, although one source snippet refers to an unnamed task simply as "a test of spatial visualization," without specifying a particular instrument.

**Links.** The only reported construct link is that spatial visualization is related to small-scale spatial cognition. The supporting text says that small-scale tasks require understanding space at the level of objects or object arrangements and evaluate skills such as spatial visualization; no causal or correlational links are reported.
