# Comprehensibility
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Understandability  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. All papers define comprehensibility as the clarity or ease with which a human can understand a model's response. The definitions differ only in wording and output modality, with most referring to generated text and one to spoken responses, so they study the same core property of model outputs.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats this construct as the clarity or ease with which model outputs can be understood by people. One definition states it as the clarity and ease of understanding of the language used in generated responses, while papers using the near-synonym Understandability describe generated text as clear and easy to comprehend by a human reader. A narrower variant keeps the same core idea but specifies spoken output, defining it as how easily a response can be comprehended when spoken aloud or by a listener.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not sharply separate Comprehensibility from Understandability, and instead use the two names for closely similar qualities of model output. One paper places Comprehensibility inside the FEEL model, alongside Informativeness, Helpfulness, Consistency, Coherence, and Safety. Beyond that, these papers do not offer a broader subtype scheme or explicit contrasts with other constructs.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is explicitly linked to this construct. Still, the snippets show it being used as a human evaluation dimension: annotators are asked to judge translations on an Understandability axis, and another paper states the dimension as how easily a spoken response can be comprehended when spoken aloud. One study also reports that outputs from simplified inputs are rated as more understandable.

**Links.** No causal, correlational, or other related links are reported for this construct.
