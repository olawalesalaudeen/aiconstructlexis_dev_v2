# Feasibility
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers here, the prevailing and only explicit definition treats feasibility as the extent to which a generated scientific hypothesis can be practically tested or validated through empirical methods. The usage is tied to judging generated research ideas and hypotheses, including references to hypotheses with "high feasibility and impact as judged by human experts" in HypER: Literature-grounded Hypothesis Generation and Distillation with Provenance.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow: papers link feasibility to one measurement instrument, Human evaluation, and to no behavioral indicators. In these studies, human evaluators assess feasibility directly alongside other dimensions of generated ideas or content, with one paper describing evaluation of the "intrinsic quality" of generated content through novelty and feasibility, and another reporting human evaluation of novelty, relevance, and feasibility.
