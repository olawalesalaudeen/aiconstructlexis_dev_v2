# Semantic drift
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** In the available usage, semantic drift is framed as a gradual divergence in meaning across successive translations, attributed to cumulative errors in lexical, syntactic, or conceptual fidelity. The same description illustrates it as meaning that "gradually shifts from a factual query to subjective concern" and as "compounding semantic drift," so the construct is presented as an iterative translation dynamic.

**Space of facets.** The papers treat semantic drift as a dynamic that unfolds over multiple translation cycles rather than breaking it into named subtypes. One paper places it alongside intrinsic sentence difficulty as part of the hidden factors influencing translation quality, and refers to degradation over iterations when discussing this dynamic.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The available operationalization instead comes from analyzing successive translation sequences over multiple cycles to produce pseudo-labels that capture the dynamics of semantic drift, with observed shifts in meaning across iterations serving as the concrete evidence.

**Links.** It is linked to translation quality and model collapse, both with related rather than causal language. One paper relates semantic drift to translation quality through analysis of multi-cycle translation sequences, and another states that model collapse is related to semantic drift, describing the two as linked in a loop of human and LLM language production.
