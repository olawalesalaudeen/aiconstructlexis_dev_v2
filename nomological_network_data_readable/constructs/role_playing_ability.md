**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the prevailing usage treats this construct as a latent ability to sustain a role during interaction. One paper defines it narrowly as adopting and consistently maintaining a specified character or persona in an interactive engagement, while another gives a broader formulation centered on maintaining character consistency, language style, emotional expression, and behavioral coherence in role-based interactions. The shared core is role maintenance in interaction, and the main difference is scope: persona consistency alone versus a wider set of expressive and behavioral consistencies.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not develop an extensive taxonomy, but one definition breaks the construct into character consistency, language style, emotional expression, and behavioral coherence. Another treats it more compactly as persona adoption and maintenance. One paper also situates role-playing alongside low-level multimodal perception, high-level cognition such as multi-step reasoning, and deliberative decision-making within a broader capability set for interactive agents.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse in the recorded relationships. Papers link the construct to one measurement instrument, Human evaluation, and no linked behavioral indicators are recorded. In the linked evidence, human evaluation is used to validate model performance, and no other linked instrument type is recorded for this construct.
