# Knowledge tracing ability
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Predictive accuracy  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the construct is framed as a latent predictive capacity: correctly forecasting a student's future performance on educational tasks from historical interaction data and evolving knowledge states. No alternative framing appears in the available papers, and the surrounding language keeps the construct tied to future-performance prediction.

**Space of facets.** The only explicit organizing move is to use the name "Predictive accuracy" for this construct, so the concept is treated in accuracy-centered terms rather than split into separate facets. In that usage, it is organized around forecasting future student performance from prior interactions and evolving knowledge states.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The available operational language, in CIKT: A Collaborative and Iterative Knowledge Tracing Framework with Large Language Models, stays at the level of predictive-performance phrasing, describing whether the model can "accurately predict future performance," "forecast student performance," and enhance "overall predictive performance," rather than naming a benchmark or evaluation procedure.

**Links.** The construct is linked to two other constructs. In COM-BOM: Bayesian Exemplar Search for Efficiently Exploring the Accuracy-Calibration Pareto Frontier, one paper claims a causal relationship from in-context learning to knowledge tracing ability, citing evidence that exemplar selection contributes more to ICL's accuracy than instruction optimization. The same paper also reports that calibration is related to knowledge tracing ability, describing exemplar selection as a joint optimization problem over accuracy and calibration with a trade-off between the two objectives.
