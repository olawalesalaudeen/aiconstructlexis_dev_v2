# Neuroticism
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the prevailing framing treats neuroticism as a personality trait, specifically one of the Big Five, centered on a tendency toward negative emotions. One definition summarizes this with examples such as anger, anxiety, or depression, while another gives a more itemized characterization through anxiety, hostility, depression, self-consciousness, impulsiveness, and vulnerability to stress. The definitions align in treating it as a trait, with the main variation being whether the description stays broad or lists specific tendencies.

**Space of facets.** Papers place neuroticism within the Big Five personality framework, alongside openness, conscientiousness, extraversion or extroversion, and agreeableness as one of five dimensions or traits. One paper also presents it through a set of characteristic tendencies, namely anxiety, hostility, depression, self-consciousness, impulsiveness, and vulnerability to stress, while another refers more generally to negative emotions.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization appears in only one direct description: one paper says it uses the IPIP-NEO-120 questionnaire to quantify behavior patterns across five dimensions including neuroticism. No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is otherwise linked to neuroticism.
