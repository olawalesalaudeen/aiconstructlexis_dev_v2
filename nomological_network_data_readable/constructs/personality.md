# Personality
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing framing treats personality as a latent, multidimensional profile or configuration of characteristic traits or patterns that shape thought, feeling, behavior, and social or emotional expression. The definitions largely align in treating personality as a latent profile rather than a single behavior, though one framing emphasizes enduring traits that affect emotions, social interaction, and responses to psychological stress, while another calls it "a multifaceted and dynamic construct" encompassing characteristic patterns of thought, feeling, and behavior.

**Space of facets.** When papers break the construct down, the recurring move is to treat it as multidimensional. One paper organizes personality with the HEXACO framework and its dimensions, while another describes it more generally as a multidimensional latent profile; no paper explicitly equates it with, nests it under, or contrasts it against other constructs.

**Space of measurement.** No paper links personality to a measurement instrument or behavioral indicator. Even so, one paper says it "utilize[s] HEXACO model ... to assess personality" and operationalizes the construct through that framework and its dimensions, while the other available usage defines personality as a latent profile but does not provide a separate instrument or behavioral indicator.

**Links.** No papers claim causal, correlational, or related links involving personality.
