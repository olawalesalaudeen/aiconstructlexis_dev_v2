# Conceptual knowledge
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, papers consistently treat conceptual knowledge as a model-internal representation of concepts rather than isolated facts. One usage describes it as the internal representation of abstract concepts and their definitions, explicitly distinct from simple factual triples, while another describes concepts as human-understandable groups of features, examples, or words that share a property and can be localized in parameters or activations.

**Space of facets.** Papers commonly organize knowledge into factual and conceptual kinds, using the contrast to mark conceptual knowledge as the more abstract category; one study says it is "more abstract and more difficult to edit than factual knowledge." Separately, one paper uses conceptualization as a related notion, linking models' ability to capture conceptual knowledge with their ability to conceptualize.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse: papers link conceptual knowledge to 1 measurement instrument, ConceptVectors, and to no behavioral indicators. ConceptVectors is described as a benchmark designed to evaluate unlearning, and one paper uses concepts collected from it in its evaluation.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, one paper reports that conceptual knowledge is related to conceptualization. Its wording connects models' ability to capture conceptual knowledge with evidence of their ability to conceptualize, and no causal or correlational links are recorded.
