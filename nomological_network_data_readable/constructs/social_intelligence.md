# Social intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 16 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Consensus. Most papers define social intelligence as a latent ability to understand and navigate complex social interactions or environments, often by inferring others' intentions or mental states, pursuing social goals, and following norms or maintaining relationships. A few definitions emphasize specific manifestations such as role simulation, collaboration, or effective social decision-making, but they still treat the construct as the same general capability in social interaction rather than a different phenomenon.

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats social intelligence as a latent capacity or ability to understand and navigate complex social situations and interactions. Most definitions add some combination of inferring others' goals or mental states, pursuing social goals, adhering to norms or social rules, maintaining relationships, or responding appropriately to others' emotions, intentions, and behaviors. A smaller line of work narrows the term to human-like role enactment and realistic collaboration, while another frames it as making effective decisions and optimizing outcomes through interaction in cooperative contexts; the main disagreement is between a broad social-navigation ability and more task-facing formulations centered on collaboration or cooperative decision-making.

**Space of facets.** Papers mostly describe multiple facets rather than fixed subtypes. Recurrent facets include social-goal pursuit, inferring or safeguarding private information, goal completion together with information or implicit reasoning, norm adherence, relationship maintenance, and sensitivity to others' emotions, intentions, and behaviors. Several papers place other constructs beneath or alongside it: theory of mind is described as a foundation of social intelligence, social reasoning as a core competency of it, and one paper asks whether general reasoning extends to this form of social intelligence; some papers also use the same term for realistic role-playing and collaboration.

**Space of measurement.** Operationally, papers link social intelligence to 2 measurement instruments and 1 behavioral indicator. The named instruments are SOTOPIA, an open-ended interactive evaluation environment for simulating complex social interactions, and ToMBench, a benchmark for emotionally and socially grounded reasoning that tests emotion recognition, social action prediction, and theory-of-mind reasoning; another paper also evaluates it through multi-turn interactions using goal completion and information reasoning, with implicit reasoning treated as a measured aspect. The only linked behavioral indicator is coalition formation, but papers relate it to the quantitative study of social intelligence rather than using it as a direct measure. These operationalizations emphasize the same elements that appear in many definitions: complex interaction, social goals, private-information or mental-state reasoning, emotion recognition, and social action prediction.

**Links.** Papers only report related links, not causal or correlational ones. They relate social intelligence to theory of mind, described as a foundation for reasoning about others' intentions, goals, thoughts, and beliefs, and to social reasoning, described as a core competency of it. Other links relate reasoning to social intelligence by asking whether existing reasoning capabilities extend to effective decision-making in cooperative contexts, and relate coalition formation to the quantitative study of social intelligence.
