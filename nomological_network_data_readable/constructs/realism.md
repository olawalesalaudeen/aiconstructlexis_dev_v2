# Realism
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats realism as how closely generated or synthetic outputs resemble the real world. One formulation defines it as the degree to which generated traffic trajectories and multi-vehicle scenarios match real-world dynamics and behaviors, while another describes it as synthetic data appearing plausible and indistinguishable from real-world data. These formulations largely agree in locating realism in the generated output and judging it against a real-world reference, with variation mainly between emphasis on behavioral resemblance and broader plausibility.

**Space of facets.** Only limited systematization is made explicit. One paper breaks realism for synthetic data into distributional similarity, where marginal and joint distributions of the synthetic table should match the original dataset, and logical consistency. Another use of the term centers on more realistic and controllable interactive trajectory generation and multi-vehicle scenarios, without further subtypes or explicit contrasts.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link realism to 1 measurement instrument, Human evaluation, and to no behavioral indicators or linked constructs. The linked instrument is a human evaluation criterion that asks whether a conversation is likely to happen in the real world from a logical perspective. This operationalization matches the broader definitions in treating realism as real-world plausibility or resemblance.
