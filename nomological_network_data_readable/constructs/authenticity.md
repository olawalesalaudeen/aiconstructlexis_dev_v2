# Authenticity
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Conflicting. Most papers use authenticity for generated text or agent behavior matching a real human, community, or individual in meaning, style, and nuance, but a substantial minority use it for something else: faithfulness of a generated article to source scientific content, or website credibility defined by the absence of deceptive practices. These are different phenomena applied to different kinds of objects, so papers using these definitions would not be studying the same thing.

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, authenticity is most commonly described as whether generated output matches a target reference, especially whether it reflects a community's meaning, content, linguistic patterns, and contextual appropriateness; related uses define it as faithfulness or credibility relative to source scientific content, or as semantic and stylistic similarity to real human utterances. A smaller but distinct usage applies the label to website credibility or originality, framed as the absence of deceptive content such as keyword overuse, duplication, clickbait, or cropping and stitching. Another distinct definition treats authenticity as an agent's behavior and self-expression aligning with the genuine, nuanced characteristics of a real individual rather than stereotypical or generic portrayals. The main disagreement is over what the construct is a property of, generated text, a website, or an agent's self-expression, and over the reference standard used.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not present a shared subtype scheme for authenticity. Instead, one paper places it alongside readability, information conveyance, and interestingness as one evaluation dimension, while the website-focused usage treats it as a binary aspect defined by deceptive versus non-deceptive content. No papers explicitly equate it with, nest it under, or contrast it against another recorded construct, and the same label is used for community-language alignment, article faithfulness, website originality or credibility, agent self-expression, and human-like utterance similarity.

**Space of measurement.** The recorded measurement link is narrow: authenticity is linked to 1 measurement instrument, Human evaluation, and to no behavioral indicators. In the linked use, judges rate authenticity as one dimension alongside readability, information conveyance, and interestingness. Other papers describe local operationalizations that follow their own definitions, including a binary judgment based on deceptive content and similarity-based scoring of user-simulator utterances with SimCSE and style embeddings, so the operationalizations vary across papers.

**Links.** No papers in the recorded relationships claim a causal, correlational, or generic related link for authenticity. The only recorded relationship is that one paper measures it with Human evaluation.
