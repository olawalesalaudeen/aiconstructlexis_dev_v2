# Linguistic diversity
**Type:** Construct  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Definition agreement:** Too little evidence.

**Space of definitions.** In the available usage, linguistic diversity is framed as a model capacity: generating or recognizing multiple valid phrasings of the same semantic content, as a reflection of natural language variation. The source text elaborates this with alternative expressions of the same idea, such as "There are 12 inches in 1 foot" and "There are 12 inches in each foot," and presents the construct as something SFT often fails to capture. No competing definition appears.

**Space of facets.** The concept is not broken into named subtypes. The only internal distinction made is between generation and recognition of alternative valid phrasings, and the discussion places linguistic diversity alongside inter-token relationships, with alternative phrasings used to illustrate "nuanced token-level dependencies" that may be overlooked.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to linguistic diversity, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The paper therefore does not specify a benchmark, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge, or probe for measuring it; instead, it illustrates the construct through examples of multiple valid expressions and describes enriched training targets as preserving "correctness and expression diversity."

**Links.** One paper claims a causal relationship from model collapse to linguistic diversity. Its evidence states that loss of the distributional tails during early model collapse can reduce linguistic innovation and cognitive diversity because of homogenized language. No correlational or related links are reported.
