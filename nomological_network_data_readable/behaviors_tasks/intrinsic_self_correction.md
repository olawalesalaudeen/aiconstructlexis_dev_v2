# Intrinsic self-correction
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, intrinsic self-correction is most commonly described as revising an initial answer by using only the problem and the model's own prior attempt, without external feedback or oracle hints. A closely aligned framing treats it as the capability to identify and fix errors from "inner knowledge" or "inherent capabilities," again without external tools or information; as one paper puts it, models "attempt to correct their initial responses without any external feedback" (Training Language Models to Self-Correct via Reinforcement Learning).

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Papers do not present a shared measurement claim about what construct this behavior evidences. The only linked construct is Reasoning, and that link is framed as related: one paper discusses intrinsic self-correction "in the context of reasoning" and reports that LLMs struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback (Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet).
