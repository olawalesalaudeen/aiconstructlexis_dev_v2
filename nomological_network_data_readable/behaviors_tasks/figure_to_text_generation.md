# Figure-to-text generation
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 7 papers  
**Also known as:** Figure captioning, Figure description, Figure summarization, Scientific figure analysis, Caption generation  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats this behavior as converting structured or visual information in a figure into natural-language text. Most formulations differ by the intended output: concise figure summaries, suitable descriptive captions, detailed descriptions, or analytical text about scientific figures. The recurring success criterion is that the text capture the figure’s information in a meaningful, descriptive, or concise way, depending on the task label.

**Variants.** Papers use several neighboring labels for this behavior, including figure summarization, figure captioning or caption generation, figure description, and scientific figure analysis. Some variants are explicitly framed around scientific figures, and one paper places captioning, summarization, and description alongside figure question answering and scientific reasoning tasks.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** In the linked operationalization, this behavior is measured by one instrument, ChartX. One paper cites ChartX under the label Figure Des., so the supporting measurement evidence is stated in figure-description terms.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The papers do not link this behavior to any construct. There is therefore no recorded agreement in these papers about what broader construct it is taken to indicate.
