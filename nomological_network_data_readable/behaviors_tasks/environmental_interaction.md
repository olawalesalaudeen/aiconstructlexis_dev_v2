# Environmental interaction
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Environment interaction, Interaction, Multi-step interaction  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed papers, the prevailing usage treats this behavior as an agent taking actions in a real or simulated environment and then receiving the resulting state, observation, or reward. Most formulations describe executing actions that change or query the environment state, while some make the goal condition explicit as achieving a target or accomplishing a task objective through those interactions. As one paper states, 'the objective of an agent A is to achieve the target G through multi-step interactions with E' (Learn-by-interact: A Data-Centric Framework For Self-Adaptive Agents in Realistic Environments).

**Variants.** Papers use several closely related names, including environmental interaction, environment interaction, interaction, and multi-step interaction. The main variants differ by setting and scope: general environment interaction, instruction-conditioned sequences of actions over multiple steps using the instruction and previous history, and game-specific interaction tasks that require finding specified entities, such as shopkeepers or altars, and interacting with them depending on context. One paper also distinguishes transitions generated from environmental interaction from those from imaginary interactions.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers do not link this behavior to any measurement instrument.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Papers do not link this behavior to any construct as what it indicates.
