# Forecasting
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Predictive analytics, Future event prediction  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, forecasting is most commonly described as generating predictions about future events or outcomes from available information, past data, and current trends. Some authors name this predictive analytics and frame it as judging "what will likely happen," while another treats future event prediction as an observable task that often takes a multiple-choice or range-based answer. Examples include predicting a future treasury yield or a future approval rating.

**Variants.** The settings vary more than the basic task. Papers mention finance and sports, business analytics uses it for predictive insights from past data, and another formulation centers on political outcome questions built from newly gathered factual data about future events. The names used for these variants include forecasting, predictive analytics, and future event prediction, and one paper also reports that forecasting is related to creative writing.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. Where elicitation is described, papers use direct questions about future outcomes, sometimes expecting a multiple-choice or range-based answer.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it as evidence of, so the available papers do not name a construct that forecasting indicates.
