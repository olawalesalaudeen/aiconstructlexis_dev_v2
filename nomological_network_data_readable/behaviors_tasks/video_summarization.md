# Video summarization
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the task is framed as producing, or supporting the production of, a summary of a video's content from frame-level evidence. The papers agree on the summary goal, but differ in how they describe the input and process: Frame-Voyager: Learning to Query Frames for Video Large Language Models emphasizes selecting evidence from frames and notes that simple frame matching can miss subtle transitions between scenes, while Video2Roleplay: A Multimodal Dataset and Framework for Video-Guided Role-playing Agents describes taking frame descriptions in video order and generating a comprehensive summary with Chain-of-Thought.

**Variants.** The available work treats it as a video-language task grounded in ordered visual evidence. One formulation centers on selecting evidence from frames, another on aggregating frame descriptions in video order, and one paper also relates video summarization to video question answering by stating that video-language tasks including summarization can be formulated as video question answering tasks.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it in the papers. There is no named elicitation or scoring instrument recorded for this behavior.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it in the papers. The available work does not explicitly present video summarization as evidence of a named construct.
