**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the available descriptions, the prevailing usage treats color recognition as identifying and naming the colors of objects in an image. The task is framed narrowly: one definition states it is "the task of identifying and naming the colors of objects in an image," and a source snippet also places it under a broader "color" question category.

**Variants.** Papers use several closely related names for this behavior, including color identification, color recognition, color, and Color Diversity. The formulations remain centered on object-level color judgments: one source treats it as a perception task, while another frames it as distinguishing and identifying items based on color.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers measure it with 2 instruments, MME-RealWorld and Waste-Bench. MME-RealWorld treats it as a perception task, and Waste-Bench operationalizes it as the question type "Color Diversity," described as testing a model's ability to distinguish and identify items based on color.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The available relationships do not link this behavior to any construct.
