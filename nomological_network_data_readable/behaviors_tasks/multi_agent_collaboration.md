# Multi-agent collaboration
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats it as multiple agents working together toward a shared task outcome. Definitions commonly describe collective deliberation through cross-examination and debate, while other descriptions stress observable coordination among specialized agents that generate a joint output to solve a task. A smaller framing casts it as producing task-oriented outputs through communication among agents and interaction with external knowledge, with success described as solving the task or answering medical questions.

**Variants.** Papers vary mainly in the interaction setting: some center collective deliberation through cross-examination and debate, some focus on coordination among specialized agents, and some describe interaction with external knowledge sources during generation. One concrete domain example is medical question answering through agent and knowledge-source interaction, and one paper explicitly labels the setup a multi-agent collaboration paradigm.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. Instead, descriptions focus on the interaction pattern itself: agents communicate, cross-examine or debate, combine specialized expertise, and produce joint, task-oriented outputs.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it, so the papers here do not present a common construct that this behavior indicates. The only recorded link is to another behavioral indicator: one paper claims a causal relationship from multi-agent collaboration to self-correction, describing a multi-agent LLM system that iteratively improves its responses for reasoning tasks with self-generated data.
