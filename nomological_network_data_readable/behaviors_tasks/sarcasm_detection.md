# Sarcasm detection
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage is as a classification task: predicting whether a text expresses sarcasm. One definition makes the criterion more explicit by describing sarcastic statements as conveying the opposite of their literal meaning, typically for humorous or critical effect, and as relying on contextual and pragmatic cues. A smaller line of work applies the same decision to spoken utterances, with papers noting that communicative intent is expressed largely through tone.

**Variants.** Papers mostly study this behavior in text settings, including sarcasm detection on the Sarcasm Headlines corpus. Another setting treats it as a speech classification task on MUSTARD clips, where the target is whether a spoken utterance is sarcastic. The common names are 'sarcasm detection' and 'sarcasm', and one paper also places it among 'instances of figurative' language.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. The papers instead describe task settings such as classification on the Sarcasm Headlines corpus and on MUSTARD clips, but no instrument is recorded as measuring it.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Construct links are limited. The only linked construct is Figurative language understanding, and one paper reports that sarcasm detection is related to it, describing sarcasm as one of the figurative language tasks used in its analyses and experiments. No other construct link is recorded.
