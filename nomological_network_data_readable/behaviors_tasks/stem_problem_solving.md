# STEM problem solving
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Chemical problem solving  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Most papers describe it as generating solutions to problems in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics domains. A common elaboration narrows this to science and mathematics questions that require applying domain principles and formulae, and one snippet adds that such questions require "deeper reasoning" and the application of physics and chemistry principles. One paper also places STEM problem solving among "out-of-distribution reasoning scenarios."

**Variants.** Papers vary it mainly by domain. Alongside broad STEM tasks, some descriptions focus on science and mathematics questions involving formulae, while a smaller line of work treats chemical problem solving as answering chemistry questions that involve calculations, unit conversions, and multi-step reasoning, including examples such as computing a threshold frequency from a work function.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers operationalize it with 3 linked instruments: GSM8K, MMLU, and Trojsten Benchmark. One study states, "We evaluate MMLU and GSM8K for STEM tasks," and another link uses Trojsten Benchmark for problem solving in Slovak STEM competition problems.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it, so papers do not present it as evidence of a named construct. Beyond measurement links, one paper claims a causal relationship from Task decomposition to STEM problem solving, describing decomposition as facilitating resolution of the original problem in a structured manner.
