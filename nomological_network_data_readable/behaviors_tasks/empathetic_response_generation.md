# Empathetic response generation
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the dominant framing is a dialogue task: producing supportive, emotionally appropriate replies that show understanding of a speaker's emotional state, often including its causal basis. Some authors broaden this to text that conveys understanding, compassion, and validation in mental health support, while another describes responses that express understanding and connection with a client's stated or inferred emotions and may encourage exploration of problems.

**Variants.** The studied variants span context- and cause-aware response generation from dialogue history, mental health support exchanges, and a psychotherapeutic setting where an AI therapist responds to a client. A smaller multimodal line conditions the response on both a client's facial image and dialogue history, and one description adds that the system should keep a consistent focus on the client's issues throughout the procedure.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. The available descriptions refer to generating subsequent empathetic responses in dialogue, but no named elicitation or scoring instrument is recorded.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** It is linked to one construct, Causal reasoning. One paper claims a causal relationship from Causal reasoning to empathetic response generation, citing declines in language quality and lexical variety when cause labels were removed; no other construct is linked to this behavior.
