# Experimental design
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Experiment design  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage is to treat this behavior as outlining an experimental setup or procedure inside a research paper in order to validate a proposed method, problem, or research idea. Most definitions describe the output as a brief experiment plan or a list of experiments, often with motivations or explanations, and some formulations make clear that this planning can be produced from partial, pre-experiment paper context and can appear even when results are simulated.

**Variants.** Papers study closely related formulations rather than sharply different versions. Some ask for a brief experiment plan and explanations from a partial paper context, while others describe outlining the setup and procedures for experiments within a paper, including cases with simulated results, or place experiment design alongside defining problems and proposing methods.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers link this behavior to one measurement instrument, Expert evaluation. The available evidence describes this as measurement through expert feedback, including evaluation via feedback from expert food scientists.
