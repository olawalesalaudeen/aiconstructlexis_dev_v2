# Speech emotion recognition
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the task is treated as identifying the emotional state expressed in human speech from audio input. The prevailing formulation places it in dialogue or conversational contexts, and in the named operationalizations success is framed as assigning emotion labels to spoken utterances.

**Variants.** The studied settings are mostly dialogue and conversational speech, including multimodal, multi-party utterances. A smaller line of usage evaluates the task under adversarial text, where one study reports that some models show "complete susceptibility to adversarial text, with accuracy dropping to precisely 0.0%."

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers link it to one measurement instrument, MELD, and no other instrument is named. MELD is described as an emotion classification benchmark, and as a multimodal multi-party dataset of conversational utterances labeled across seven emotion categories; another description adds that it includes sentiment annotation.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The linked papers do not connect this behavior to any construct, so they do not specify what broader attribute it is taken to indicate.
