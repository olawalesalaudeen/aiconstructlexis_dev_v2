# Critique
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 13 papers  
**Also known as:** Mistake identification, Self-critique generation  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers here, critique is used in two main ways rather than one prevailing formulation. In tutoring work, it is framed as recognizing a student's error, including whether "the tutor identified/recognized a mistake in a student's response" and whether "a good-quality response from the tutor should include the relevant mistake identification" (Unifying AI Tutor Evaluation: An Evaluation Taxonomy for Pedagogical Ability Assessment of LLM-Powered AI Tutors). Another paper uses it as self-critique generation, meaning text that identifies and explains potential flaws or errors in the model's own previous reasoning or response.

**Variants.** The main variants are mistake identification and self-critique generation. The tutoring variant concerns errors in a student's reasoning or calculation, while the self-critique variant concerns flaws in the model's own previously generated reasoning or response.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. The available descriptions instead state the behavior directly through response criteria, especially whether a tutor recognizes a student's mistake and includes the relevant mistake identification in its reply.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it, so the papers do not provide a recorded account of what broader construct this behavior is taken to indicate.
