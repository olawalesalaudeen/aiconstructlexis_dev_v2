# Word sorting
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the dominant framing treats word sorting as the task of arranging a given list of words in lexicographical, alphabetical order. Some authors state the rule more generally, as arranging words according to a specified criterion or the task's required sorting rule, but they still describe the behavior as producing an ordered list of words.

**Variants.** Papers usually refer to the behavior simply as Word Sorting, and one source abbreviates it as WS. The main variation is in how the ordering rule is stated: most commonly lexicographical ordering, with a smaller wording that leaves the criterion task-specific. Papers also place the task in BIG-bench reasoning-task settings, and one paper additionally lists it among BIG-Bench Hard tasks.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers measure this behavior with two instruments: most commonly BIG-bench, and also BIG-Bench Hard. In these uses, word sorting is presented as one of the reasoning tasks evaluated in BIG-Bench, while another paper includes it as one of the tasks in BIG-Bench Hard. No other measurement instrument is linked to it.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to word sorting in the papers, so the available literature does not specify what broader construct performance on this task is taken to indicate.
