# Moral judgment
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Ethical judgment  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats moral judgment as scenario-based evaluation of whether an action is morally acceptable, or as assigning blame and judging right and wrong in morally ambiguous interpersonal situations. One example asks models to judge "whether stealing the bread would be morally acceptable" (Eliciting Human Preferences with Language Models). Some authors also describe it as a latent tendency that reflects underlying value systems and sensitivity to moral principles, while a smaller framing casts it as ethical judgment, the observable task of evaluating text under principles such as deontology or justice.

**Variants.** Papers study it in several formulations: morally ambiguous interpersonal scenarios, direct acceptability judgments about described actions, and ethical judgment tasks labeled deontology and justice. One paper also examines persona-conditioned moral decision making, asking how personas prioritize authority, loyalty, fairness, care, liberty, and sanctity, and reporting that assigned personas affect blaming tendencies (Synthetic Socratic Debates: Examining Persona Effects on Moral Decision and Persuasion Dynamics).

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. In the available descriptions, elicitation is mainly scenario based: models are asked to judge whether an action is morally acceptable, and one paper states that models are evaluated on their ability to predict human judgments about those scenarios. Another description frames the task as evaluating text according to principles such as deontology or justice.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it in the available papers. Although one definition says the behavior reflects underlying value systems and sensitivity to moral principles, the papers do not make an explicit construct-level link.
