# Scientific problem solving
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Definitions consistently treat this as work on complex problems drawn from scientific or natural science domains. The prevailing formulation is end to end: a model receives such a problem and, in the more specific wording, is expected to produce a complete, functional coded solution; a broader wording describes it as addressing complex scientific-domain problems that require structured reasoning and domain knowledge.

**Variants.** The available uses emphasize natural science settings and scientific research problems. One line of work frames it narrowly as code generation for real scientific research problems and reports problems from 16 diverse domains, while another places it more broadly among challenging tasks alongside mathematics and code generation.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers link it to two measurement instruments, OlympiadBench and SciBench. The recorded uses say it is measured by OlympiadBench in work on scientific problems, and by SciBench, which is described as assessing college-level scientific problem-solving abilities.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The linked construct claims do not explicitly present this behavior as evidence of a construct. Instead, one paper claims causal relationships from Scientific knowledge and from Scientific reasoning to Scientific problem solving, stating that performance improves when background is provided and reading weaker results as lack of knowledge and reasoning capability in natural science fields; these are the only linked constructs.
