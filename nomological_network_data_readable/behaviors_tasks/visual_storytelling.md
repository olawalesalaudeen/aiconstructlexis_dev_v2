# Visual storytelling
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 8 papers  
**Also known as:** Visual story telling  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats visual storytelling as generating a coherent narrative or story from a sequence of images. Most papers keep this sequence-to-story framing, though some narrow the task to story completion, asking for a concluding narrative sentence from preceding images and captions plus a last image, and another paper describes a narrative story accompanied by relevant images in an interleaved format. One paper instead states it as generating a narrative or story based on the content of an image.

**Variants.** Papers use the names visual storytelling, visual story telling, and VST. The main variants are full story generation from image sequences, interleaved text-and-image storytelling, and finishing a story from earlier image-caption pairs and a final image. In related usage, one paper claims a causal relationship from interleaved text-and-image generation to visual storytelling, saying it "opens up possibilities" for this application.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers link visual storytelling to one measurement instrument, VIST, and no other instrument is linked to it. The paper that links it to VIST refers to "visual storytelling from VIST" and does not specify further elicitation or scoring details.
