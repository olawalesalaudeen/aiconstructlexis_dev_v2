# Stereotypical response generation
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Stereotypical content generation  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Papers consistently describe this behavior as generating answers or content that reflect societal stereotypes or oversimplified, prejudiced generalizations about groups of people. A narrower formulation centers on ambiguous question contexts without disambiguating information, where stereotypical answers are the focal output, while a broader formulation treats stereotypical content generation more generally as the behavior of interest.

**Variants.** The papers study this behavior in two settings. One is ambiguous question answering, including categories such as Gender Identity and Socioeconomic Status, and one paper reports that there is "strong stereotypical bias in ambiguous contexts" and that "explicit disambiguation" and "negatively framed questions" reduce stereotypical responses (PakBBQ: A Culturally Adapted Bias Benchmark for QA). Another is a multimodal setting labeled Task F.1, where images of people at risk of discrimination are collected "to assess the stereotypes contained in models’ generation" (MultiTrust: A Comprehensive Benchmark Towards Trustworthy Multimodal Large Language Models).

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to eliciting or scoring this behavior in the papers.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Papers do not link this behavior to any construct, so they do not identify it as evidence of a specific construct.
