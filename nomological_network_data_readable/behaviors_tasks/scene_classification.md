# Scene classification
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Scene recognition  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage is identifying and classifying the overall scene, environment, or setting depicted in an image. Most definitions treat success as recognizing the image-level scene or assigning a semantic label to the whole image based on its overall content, with examples such as "harbor" or "residential area." Another common phrasing is simply how well models can identify and classify different scenes or environments.

**Variants.** Papers use both "scene classification" and "scene recognition" for this behavior. The settings mentioned include remote sensing image understanding, where the whole image is labeled by scene type, and a more localized instruction that asks a model to "Highlight the area that shows a natural outdoor scene." One paper also includes it as a question category.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it in the papers.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it in the papers, so the papers do not specify what it is evidence of.
