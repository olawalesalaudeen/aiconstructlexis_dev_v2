**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Most commonly, papers treat puzzle solving as finding a solution to a defined puzzle or problem, especially classic puzzles, number games, and word games posed under custom rules and constraints. The prevailing formulation emphasizes rule-governed problem solving rather than open-ended generation: one benchmark asks models to "solve puzzles and intellectual games based on newly defined problem-solving frameworks" (KOR-Bench: Benchmarking Language Models on Knowledge-Orthogonal Reasoning Tasks). A smaller line of work uses a broader phrasing, calling it the observable behavior of finding a solution to a defined puzzle or problem.

**Variants.** The main variants are classic paper puzzles, number games, and word games, which one paper explicitly names as the rule categories for its puzzle section. Another variant is multi-image puzzle solving, framed as solving puzzles that require understanding and reasoning across multiple images. Some work also studies puzzles in a centaur evaluation setting with crowdworkers, where they appear alongside collaborative writing and summarization tasks.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to puzzle solving in the papers.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to puzzle solving in the papers.
