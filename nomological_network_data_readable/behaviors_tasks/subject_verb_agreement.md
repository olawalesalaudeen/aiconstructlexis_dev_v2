# Subject-verb agreement
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** Subject-verb agreement judgment  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, subject-verb agreement is most commonly framed as a task with a correct output: either choosing the verb inflection that matches the subject's grammatical number, or producing the correct acceptability judgment for sentences built around that contrast. The definitions align on the core criterion, getting the agreement pattern right, though they differ in output format, inflection choice in one line of work and acceptability judgment in another; the judgment formulation is described particularly for sentences with attractor nouns.

**Variants.** Papers use both the name "subject-verb agreement" and the narrower "subject-verb agreement judgment," and one study abbreviates it as "S-v agr." A smaller line of work focuses on agreement judgments in sentences with attractor nouns, including cases where "attractors in a relative clause hinder acceptability judgments," and illustrates the contrast with examples such as "The sisters bake/*bakes." Another paper refers more broadly to a "suite of subject-verb agreement tasks."

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to this behavior. Instead, the papers operationalize it directly through the task formats they describe, namely choosing the appropriate verb inflection or giving acceptability judgments for agreement sentences.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The linked construct usage is narrow: papers connect this behavior only to syntactic competence. One paper states that syntactic competence is measured by "determiner-noun agreement, subject-verb agreement, and gender and number agreement across anaphoric chains," so subject-verb agreement is used there as a measure of syntactic competence.
