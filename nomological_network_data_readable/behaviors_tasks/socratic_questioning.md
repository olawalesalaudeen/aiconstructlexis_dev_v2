# Socratic questioning
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** Socratic dialogue  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, it is treated as inquiry-based questioning that gets the learner to work toward an answer rather than receive it directly. One formulation in "MathTutorBench: A Benchmark for Measuring Open-ended Pedagogical Capabilities of LLM Tutors" narrows this to generating, for each correct solution step, at least one corresponding guidance question that could be posed instead of simply giving the answer. Another paper uses the label "Socratic dialogue" for open-ended questioning that encourages exploration and self-reflection rather than direct correction.

**Variants.** The papers use closely related names, "Socratic questioning" and "Socratic dialogue," and study them in different tutoring settings. One focuses on math problem solving as step-aligned guidance questions tied to correct solution steps, while the other discusses English education and contrasts Socratic dialogue with straightforward Q&A by emphasizing open-ended, iterative exploration.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. The only explicit operationalization is an evaluation task in which a model is checked for producing at least one guidance question for each correct solution step, with success defined by asking toward the correct answer instead of simply providing it.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to this behavior in the papers.
