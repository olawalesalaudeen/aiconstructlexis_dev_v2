# Narrative summarization
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the cited work, the prevailing usage treats narrative summarization as producing a condensed version of a narrative that distills the "key details and takeaways" from the source. One paper explicitly adds that this process can involve interpretive choices and subjectivity, while another defines the task specifically for long-form narratives and says the summary should capture intricate plotlines, character interactions, and thematic coherence.

**Variants.** Papers use the behavior for both general stories and explicitly long-form narratives. The named long-form domains are books, movies, and TV scripts, and this framing presents the task as summarizing extended narrative texts rather than short passages.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** The papers do not link narrative summarization to any measurement instrument. One paper does describe a summary as "a collection of claims" that can be obtained through a decomposition process, but no named instrument is reported as eliciting or scoring the behavior.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The papers do not link narrative summarization to any construct, so they do not explicitly treat it as evidence of a broader construct.
