# Coherent text generation
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 7 papers  
**Also known as:** Coherent response generation, Incoherent generation, Discourse coherence  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats this behavior as generating text or responses that are logical, consistent, and easy to understand. A related framing focuses on discourse coherence, meaning logical and thematic consistency across sentences or paragraphs. A smaller line of work uses the inverse label, incoherent generation, for outputs that lack logical, semantic, or syntactic consistency, including repetitive or nonsensical text.

**Variants.** Papers use nearby names such as coherent response generation, discourse coherence, and incoherent generation. The behavior appears in instruction following and in tasks involving coherence across sentences or paragraphs. The inverse variant is illustrated with outputs that become repetitive, repeat a single word, produce nonsensical sequences, or fail to generate text.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. The only scoring detail stated directly is that one paper uses "win-rates" to gauge coherent language generation capabilities.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it, and the papers do not record it as evidence of any particular construct.
