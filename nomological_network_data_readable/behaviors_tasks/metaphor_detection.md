# Metaphor detection
**Type:** Behavior  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the available definitions, the prevailing usage treats this behavior as sentence-level identification of whether a text contains a metaphor, with success framed as assigning the corresponding metaphor label. The task is stated directly as prediction: for a given sentence, the system is asked to output the metaphor label, and one study says that “our goal is to predict the corresponding hyperbole label yh and metaphor label ym” (Enhancing Hyperbole and Metaphor Detection with Their Bidirectional Dynamic Interaction and Emotion Knowledge).

**Variants.** The only explicit variant is text-based detection on a given sentence. Papers also study it alongside nearby figurative-language tasks, especially hyperbole detection, but no further sub-tasks, modalities, or alternative judging procedures are specified.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. In the available descriptions, elicitation and scoring are built directly into the task formulation: a sentence is presented and the output is the predicted metaphor label.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The only linked construct is Figurative language understanding, and the recorded relationship is related. In that usage, metaphors are grouped with other language-understanding tasks, with one paper describing “two types of figurative language (sarcasm and metaphors)” in its analyses and experiments (Class Distillation with Mahalanobis Contrast: An Efficient Training Paradigm for Pragmatic Language Understanding Tasks).
