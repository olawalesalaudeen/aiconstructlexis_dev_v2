# HarmBench
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 69 papers  
**Also known as:** Harmbench, HARMBENCH  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, HarmBench is most commonly described as a benchmark or dataset of harmful instructions, questions, or behaviors for evaluating jailbreak success and safety behavior under adversarial attacks. Several definitions specify a standard set of 200 harmful behaviors or instructions, including tasks such as chemical synthetization, cybercrime, misinformation, harassment, and physically harmful behaviors, while one paper treats it as an out-of-distribution test set distinct from AdvBench and others report using 400 questions, 300 malicious queries, 100 sampled harmful questions, 80 cases, or a 27-point subset from JBB-Behaviors. A smaller line of descriptions foregrounds the evaluation protocol rather than the prompt set, referring to an official HarmBench classifier, a default judge, or automated assessment of jailbreak success with GPT-3.5-turbo.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link HarmBench to 6 constructs and 5 behavioral indicators. The most prevalent reported behavioral use is to measure jailbreaking, often through attack success rate and harm judges or classifiers; among constructs, papers most often use it to measure harmlessness, adversarial robustness, and safety, and they also use it for harmful request refusal. Taken together, these measurement claims are distributed across multiple targets rather than tied to one construct.

**Space of uses.** Papers frequently use HarmBench alongside AdvBench, and some pair it with JailbreakBench or Do-Not-Answer. In those settings it is usually positioned as a standard or comprehensive benchmark for jailbreaking and safety evaluation, sometimes as a fixed evaluation set and sometimes as an out-of-distribution check. Several papers also rely on its judging setup directly, using the official classifier, a LLaMA-2-based harm classifier, or the default HarmBench judge to score attack success or whether a response is harmful.

**Concerns raised.** A recurring concern is data leakage. Papers therefore emphasize that some HarmBench evaluations are "not involved in the training process", use it as a set distinct from AdvBench for out-of-distribution testing, or restrict evaluation to a 27-point subset to prevent data leakage. One paper also excludes copyright-related behaviors because it says they should be evaluated differently.
