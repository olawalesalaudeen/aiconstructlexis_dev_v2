**Type:** Measurement

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage is a reading comprehension or multiple-choice question answering benchmark for logical reasoning. Definitions commonly say it is sourced or derived from standardized tests, especially LSAT and GMAT, or from American graduate school entrance exams and their preparatory materials. Some authors describe it more specifically as the Reading Comprehension with Logical Reasoning dataset, and some descriptions frame it as assessing the ability to draw correct inferences from complex textual premises or to perform logical reasoning from provided text.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Across papers, the measurement claims converge on 1 construct, Logical reasoning, and no behavioral indicators are linked to it. Papers use it as a logical reasoning dataset or benchmark, including descriptions that they use ReClor for logical reasoning and for overall comparisons on logic tasks. The measurement claims therefore center on a single construct rather than spreading across multiple constructs.

**Space of uses.** Papers commonly include it alongside other benchmarks, for example ARC and CSQA in natural language reasoning question-answering experiments, LogiQA for logical reasoning, and MMLU in broader evaluation lists. One paper also applies it to test a method's applicability or transferability to general logic tasks. No measurement instrument is linked to it.
