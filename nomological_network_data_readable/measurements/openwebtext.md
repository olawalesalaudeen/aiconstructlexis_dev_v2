# OpenWebText
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 32 papers  
**Also known as:** Openwebtext, OPENWEBTEXT  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, OpenWebText is most commonly described as a large-scale English or web text corpus, often framed as an open-source replication of WebText for pre-training and validation. Some definitions add that it is scraped from the web, and they differ slightly on size, describing it as approximately 8 billion web-crawled tokens or 9 billion tokens. A smaller line of work treats it more narrowly as a source of prompts for sentence continuation or as a text generation dataset for open-ended language completion.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link OpenWebText to one behavioral indicator, Text generation, and one construct, Generalization. The prevailing measurement claim centers on Text generation: papers use OpenWebText to compare sample quality or to evaluate "text generation on the OpenWebText (OWT) dataset" (Speculative Diffusion Decoding: Accelerating Language Generation through Diffusion). A smaller line of work uses it to measure Generalization, with one paper evaluating bounds on OpenWebText.

**Space of uses.** Most often, papers position OpenWebText as a pretraining corpus for GPT-2 series or autoregressive language models, sometimes training from scratch, and also as a validation or evaluation setting through validation perplexity, sentence continuation prompts, or an OpenWebText task. It is used alongside The Pile, WIKITEXT103, WebText, and Amber in parallel training, comparison, or evaluation setups. One paper explicitly says, "We consider a dataset of prompts from OpenWebText" for a sentence continuation setup (Amortizing intractable inference in large language models).
