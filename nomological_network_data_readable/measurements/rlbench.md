# RLBench
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, RLBench is most commonly treated as a simulated, and in one definition large-scale, benchmark and learning environment for robotic manipulation. Definitions vary in emphasis: some describe tabletop tasks and a Franka robot performing both prehensile and non-prehensile tasks, while others frame it as a benchmark for language-conditioned or language-guided robotic manipulation. Papers also describe it as being used to generate trajectories and to evaluate manipulation-related path prediction or other challenging simulation tasks.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link RLBench to one behavioral indicator, robotic manipulation, and to no constructs. The recorded measurement claim is concentrated rather than spread across multiple targets: RLBench is used to measure robotic manipulation, with the supporting description referring to tabletop manipulation in simulation.

**Space of uses.** Across the papers, RLBench is positioned as a simulation benchmark or learning environment rather than being linked to other measurement instruments. Authors use it for evaluation, including generating trajectories or episodes and assessing model performance on manipulation tasks, and one paper places it alongside CALVIN and VLMBench among benchmarks for language-guided robotic manipulation.
