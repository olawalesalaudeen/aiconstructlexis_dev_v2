## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats CUB-200-2011, also written as CUB-200, CUB, CaltechUCSD Bird-200, Birds-200-2011, and Caltech-UCSD Birds, as a fine-grained bird image benchmark or dataset for 200 bird species. Most definitions frame it as an image classification or fine-grained visual classification benchmark, often within FGVC, while some papers also describe it as a fine-grained image dataset used for image captioning and generation, or as a source of bird attributes for evaluating attribute relativity in the animal domain. A smaller line of work describes it in terms of detailed visual understanding, localization, and concept-based evaluation.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link this instrument to 5 behavioral indicators and 1 construct. The dominant measurement claim is image classification, while smaller lines of work use it to measure cross-modal retrieval, image captioning, image reconstruction, and object recognition. Papers also report it as measuring interpretability and explainability, with one study stating that CUB offers image-level concept annotations that enable quantitative evaluation.

**Space of uses.** Papers usually use it as one dataset in broader benchmark suites, alongside Stanford Dogs, FGVC-Aircraft, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Caltech101, among others, rather than as a standalone protocol. The only linked instrument is FGVC, where papers describe CUB-200-2011 as one of the fine-grained visual classification datasets in that suite. Depending on the study, it is used for zero-shot or linear-probe image classification, finetuning, training concept-based models, or as the dataset for captioning, reconstruction, and retrieval cases.
