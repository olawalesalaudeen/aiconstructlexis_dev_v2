# M3Exam
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  
**Also known as:** M3EXAM  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers here, M3Exam is most commonly described as a multitask, multilingual benchmark used to evaluate multimodal models. A narrower description in Is Translation All You Need? A Study on Solving Multilingual Tasks with Large Language Models treats it as a multilingual, multi-subject, multiple-choice exam benchmark built from real-world national exam questions, with questions drawn from different languages rather than translated from English.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link M3Exam to 1 construct and no behavioral indicators, so the reported measurement claims converge on a single construct rather than spreading across many targets. Its linked construct is Cultural knowledge: one paper uses M3Exam to measure it, stating that the questions require strong multilingual proficiency and cultural knowledge to perform well.

**Space of uses.** No measurement instrument is linked to it in the recorded relationships. In use, papers present M3Exam as an evaluation benchmark or dataset: R2-MultiOmnia: Leading Multilingual Multimodal Reasoning via Self-Training uses it together with XMMMLU and MAXM to study multimodal reasoning performance, while another paper selects it because its questions are real-world natural data from different languages instead of English translations.
