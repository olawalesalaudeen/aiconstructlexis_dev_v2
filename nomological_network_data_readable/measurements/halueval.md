# HaluEval
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 16 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, HaluEval is most commonly described as a large-scale benchmark or dataset for evaluating hallucination, often factual hallucination, in model-generated responses. Some papers narrow this to language-only dialogue contexts or to question answering, including judging whether an answer contains a hallucination with yes/no accuracy, while others use dialogue and summarization subsets called HaluDial and HaluSum. Papers variously describe its contents as synthetically generated hallucination data, as user queries paired with factual and hallucinated LLM responses annotated by human evaluators, and, for a QA track, as 10000 questions with reference knowledge, a right answer, and a hallucinated answer automatically generated by ChatGPT.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use HaluEval to measure 4 constructs and 3 behavioral indicators. The prevailing use is to measure Hallucination, with additional uses for Hallucination detection, Text summarization, Generalization, and Factuality. Overall, the linked claims span several targets rather than one construct, although hallucination-focused uses are the most common.

**Space of uses.** In practice, HaluEval is frequently used alongside TruthfulQA, either as a companion benchmark for factual reasoning or as a transfer target when directions, hyperparameters, or models learned on TruthfulQA are applied to it. Smaller lines of work place it beside HalluQA, BamBoo, and SelfCheckGPT-WikiBio, and one paper contrasts it with TruthfulQA Generation as a discriminative benchmark. Papers use it both as a direct evaluation set, including dialogue and summarization subsets, and as a source of instances for calculating editing projections before evaluation on another benchmark.
