# RiddleSense
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 3 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the cited papers, RiddleSense is most commonly described as a benchmark dataset of riddle-style questions for evaluating commonsense reasoning. The prevailing wording presents it as complex commonsense reasoning in the framework of riddle-style, multiple-choice questions, while another definition more generally calls it a commonsense reasoning benchmark involving riddles. Some authors also place it among lateral thinking-related benchmarks.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use RiddleSense to measure a single construct, Commonsense reasoning, and no behavioral indicators are linked to it. The claims converge on that construct rather than spreading across several: it is described as evaluating complex commonsense reasoning, including in out-of-domain evaluation settings.

**Space of uses.** Papers position RiddleSense mainly as an existing benchmark used in broader benchmark comparisons and out-of-domain evaluation. It is analyzed alongside BiRdQA and BrainTeaser in benchmark analysis, included with Story Cloze Test, HellaSwag, and NumerSense in out-of-domain commonsense reasoning evaluation, and cited as a lateral thinking-related benchmark to which another benchmark's data and reasoning processes may be applied. No measurement instrument is linked to it.

**Concerns raised.** A concern raised in some work is answer ambiguity. It reports a non-trivial amount of answer ambiguity in RiddleSense when analyzing existing benchmarks.
