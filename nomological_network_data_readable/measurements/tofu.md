# TOFU
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 23 papers  
**Also known as:** TOFU dataset  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most commonly, papers describe TOFU as a synthetic question-answering benchmark for unlearning, built around fictitious author profiles or biographies. Several descriptions specify 200 synthetic authors or profiles with 20 question-answer pairs each, totaling 4,000 items, while another description instead says it consists of 20 question-answer pairs based on fictitious author biographies generated by GPT-4, and one paper frames it around fictional characters' personal information. Papers also refer to benchmark subsets and splits such as forget05, forget10 with retain90, and Retain10-perturbed, and one definition says it evaluates different forget set sizes of 1%, 5%, and 10%.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Across relationship claims, TOFU is used to measure 3 constructs and 1 behavioral indicator, with claims spread across several targets rather than tied to only one. The most common claims are that it measures General ability, Forget quality, and Verbatim memorization; papers describe using TOFU's retain and forget splits, model utility datasets, forget quality metrics, and Retain10-perturbed Truth Ratio evaluations for these purposes. A smaller line of work uses TOFU to measure Text generation through fictitious knowledge generation questions about fake authors.

**Space of uses.** In usage, papers usually place TOFU inside broader benchmark suites, repeatedly mentioning it alongside WMDP, MUSE, and the Harry Potter series or Who's Harry Potter. It is linked to 1 other instrument, MUSE, as a related benchmark, and one paper contrasts the two by describing TOFU as evaluating unlearning on synthetic Q&A datasets. Across these papers, TOFU is positioned as a popular or well-established unlearning benchmark for comparative experiments.

**Concerns raised.** One paper contrasts TOFU with MUSE by noting that TOFU evaluates unlearning on synthetic Q&A datasets, whereas MUSE tackles real-world unlearning challenges.
