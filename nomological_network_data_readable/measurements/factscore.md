# FActScore
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 35 papers  
**Also known as:** FactScore, FActscore, FACTSCORE, FACTSCORES, FactScore-Bio  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the prevailing description is a factuality evaluation procedure or benchmark for long-form generation, especially biographies, that breaks outputs into atomic facts or claims and verifies each one against a knowledge source, often Wikipedia or retrieved passages. Most definitions present it as a fine-grained, reference-based method that reports the percentage or proportion of supported atomic facts, a precision score, or the numbers of true and false facts. Some papers describe it more specifically as an automated retrieval augmentation pipeline, a suite and fact evaluator for faithful biography generation, or a decomposition policy with annotated demonstrations; a smaller set of definitions also refer to FACTSCORES as citation-supported sub-facts and to FactScore-Bio as a long-form QA dataset of biography questions from Wikipedia.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link it to 2 constructs and 3 behavioral indicators. The prevailing use is to measure Factuality, with papers using it as a representative reference-based truthfulness score, to evaluate factual long-form summaries and biographies, and to assess the factuality of model-generated responses. A smaller line of work uses it to measure Faithfulness, including faithful biography generation and factual consistency of condensations, while single-paper links report it as measuring Biography generation, Hallucination, and Claim decomposition, so the claims are concentrated on factuality rather than spread evenly across constructs.

**Space of uses.** Across papers it is usually positioned as an evaluation benchmark, procedure, or automated pipeline, and it is applied to biographies, long-form generation, generated summaries, condensations, edited model outputs, and out-of-domain evaluation. Papers use it alongside 1 linked instrument, Biography, and also alongside TruthfulQA, SAFE, FAITHSCORE, and VERISCORE in comparison or joint evaluation settings. Some papers frame it as a headline benchmark, while others use the FactScore suite or decomposition engine and verifier as an auxiliary evaluator.
