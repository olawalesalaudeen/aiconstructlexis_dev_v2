# AIR-Bench
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 12 papers  
**Also known as:** AirBench, AIR-bench  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed papers, the prevailing usage treats AIR-Bench as a benchmark or aggregated evaluation suite for large audio or audio-language models, aimed at audio understanding in a relatively holistic way. Most descriptions place its content across speech, audio, and music, and one survey specifically describes it as an open-ended question-answering benchmark for general auditory processing. Papers also mention named components or tracks within it, including AirBench-Chat for conversational ability and a foundation track for evaluating foundation models; one separate usage describes AIR-Bench instead as a retrieval benchmark whose test data is automatically generated by large language models.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use AIR-Bench to evaluate a spread of targets rather than a single recorded construct. In descriptions and snippets it is reported as evaluating audio understanding, audio comprehension, general auditory processing, conversational ability in AirBench-Chat, and, in one paper, retrieval performance; another paper highlights its foundation track for evaluating foundation models. No constructs and no behavioral indicators are linked to it.

**Space of uses.** In use, AIR-Bench is usually positioned alongside other broad evaluation resources rather than as a standalone instrument. The only recorded instrument link is that it is related to AudioBench, and papers discuss the two together as aggregated evaluation sets for LAMs and as benchmarks that extend to open-ended QA formats; one source also groups it with VoiceBench, and a retrieval paper reports results on AIR-Bench together with MTEB.

**Concerns raised.** The concerns that appear are about modality coverage and task breadth. One paper says that although AirBench can assess spoken language comprehension, its conversational evaluation in AirBench-Chat is based on text-based interactions and "does not address spoken dialogue capabilities"; another says the foundation track offers a limited number of tasks for evaluating universal models.
