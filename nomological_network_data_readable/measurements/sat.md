# SAT
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across these papers, the prevailing description is of SAT as a standardized test used to evaluate model performance. A smaller framing narrows this to a set of math problems from the SAT used for quantitative reasoning, with one paper noting that this subset contains 32 four-choice problems.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Claims about what SAT measures are concentrated rather than spread out: it is linked to one construct, Mathematical reasoning, and to no behavioral indicators. One paper explicitly treats Mathematical reasoning as measured by SAT, and another definition describes the instrument as used to evaluate quantitative reasoning.

**Space of uses.** Papers use SAT as an evaluation instrument for model performance, with one paper placing it among standardized tests on which GPT-4 scores are described as comparable to those of human test-takers. Another paper includes it alongside GSM8k, MATH, GSM-Hard, SVAMP, and ASDiv, among other mathematical reasoning benchmarks, and reports SAT results by averaging over the last three checkpoints when available.

**Concerns raised.** The only stated limitation is its scale and format: one paper says the SAT subset "only has 32 four-choice problems," which is why it averages results over the last three checkpoints when available.
