## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the prevailing framing is a large-scale image classification or object recognition benchmark, usually under the names ImageNet, ImageNet-1K, ImageNet1k, ImageNet-1k, or ImageNet-2012. Definitions commonly specify 1,000 categories or classes, and some add scale details such as over 1.2 million images, or over a million training images plus 50,000 validation images. Although most uses are classification-oriented, some papers also treat it as a standard class-conditional image generation setting, and a few rely on narrower slices or transformations such as the ILSVRC2012 validation split or flattened grayscale image patches.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use it to measure 3 constructs and 5 behavioral indicators. The dominant link is image classification, with many papers using it for classification evaluation, sometimes explicitly through top-1 accuracy or core classification accuracy. Smaller strands report it as measuring multimodal alignment, image generation, adaptability, and generalization, plus 3 other linked behavioral indicators: object recognition, synthetic recall, and zero-shot classification. Some alignment-oriented work also states that ImageNet top-1 accuracy is used alongside reconstruction FID to assess "reconstruction and text alignment capabilities."

**Space of uses.** Most papers position it as a headline evaluation benchmark for vision or vision-language models, often for classification accuracy, though some also train or pre-train models or auxiliary components on it. It is often used alongside broader suites such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, MS-COCO, Caltech101, and Flowers102, with several other vision datasets also appearing. A smaller set repurposes it to build downstream artifacts, including fine-grained VQA tasks, structured rationales for all 1,000 categories, target image sets and attack texts from ILSVRC2012 validation labels, and flattened grayscale patches for compression experiments.
