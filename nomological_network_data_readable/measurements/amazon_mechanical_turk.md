# Amazon Mechanical Turk
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 8 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats Amazon Mechanical Turk as a crowdsourcing platform for collecting human annotations and human evaluations. Most definitions describe it as a platform, while one frames it as a crowdsourced evaluation procedure; the reported uses include collecting valid versus invalid labels to train critic models, rating generated plans, comparing outputs on fluency and descriptiveness, evaluating model outputs across a test set, building an annotation platform for splitting and labeling interaction sequences, verifying benchmark samples and human performance, and collecting open-ended answers and ratings for visual riddle tasks.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Across these papers, Amazon Mechanical Turk is not explicitly linked to named constructs or behavioral indicators. Instead, it is used to gather task-specific human judgments and annotations, including fluency and descriptiveness comparisons, ratings of generated plans, subjective ratings on generated diagrams, human verification and human performance on image-caption matching tasks, and human answers and ratings for visual riddles.

**Space of uses.** Papers position Amazon Mechanical Turk as a means of collecting task-specific annotations and human judgments. It is used for training critic models from human labels, comparative human evaluation of model outputs, comprehensive test-set annotation, benchmark verification and human baseline collection, and answer collection for visual riddle tasks; no other measurement instrument is linked to it.
