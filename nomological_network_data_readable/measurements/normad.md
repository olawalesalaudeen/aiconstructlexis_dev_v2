# NormAd
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** NORMAD  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed papers, NORMAD is most commonly described as a benchmark or hierarchical evaluation framework for LLM cultural adaptability, though one paper also refers to it as a dataset. Definitions converge on a multi-country set of free-text or short social situations, reported as 2,600 stories from 75 countries, where a character either adheres to or violates a cultural norm; models judge the acceptability of these situations under different levels of cultural context specificity, from abstract values to explicit social norms and etiquette.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use NORMAD to measure Cultural adaptation, and this is the only linked construct. It is linked to one construct and no behavioral indicators, so the reported measurement claims converge on cultural adaptation, including judging social acceptability across varying levels of cultural norm specificity.

**Space of uses.** Papers position NORMAD mainly as a direct evaluation setup for studying cultural adaptability in LLMs. One paper introduces it as a multi-level framework for social acceptability judgments, and another reports using the NORMAD dataset "to study cultural adaptability in LLMs"; no other measurement instrument is linked to it.
