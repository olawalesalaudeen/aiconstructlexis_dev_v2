# C4
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 110 papers  
**Also known as:** c4 en-validation, C4 validation loss, C4 validation set, C4 Corpus  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, C4 is most commonly treated as a large, cleaned web-text corpus derived from Common Crawl, sometimes described more specifically as English text and sometimes as a general-domain dataset. The prevailing use is as a language-modeling benchmark where papers report perplexity or loss, though some papers also present it as calibration data for quantization or pruning, as pretraining or continual-training data, as a source of prompts for open-ended generation, and as material for neuron-activation analysis. Papers also refer to variants and subsets such as the en subset, c4 en-validation, the C4 validation set or C4 validation loss, the C4 Corpus, the first shard, and subsets of validation data.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link C4 to four constructs and one behavioral indicator. The most recurring reported use is to measure Text generation, with several papers evaluating it on C4, usually through perplexity; individual papers also report C4 as measuring Generative ability, Language modeling ability, Length generalization, and Quantization robustness. Overall, the measurement claims are spread across several targets rather than concentrated on a single construct.

**Space of uses.** No measurement instrument is linked to C4 in relationship claims, although papers often use it alongside WikiText2 and PTB or Penn Treebank in perplexity evaluations, and more occasionally alongside ELI5, Dbpedia Class, or PubMed in task-specific setups. A recurring pattern is dual use: papers sample calibration data or 2048-token segments from C4 and also report results on C4 validation data, while other papers use it as pretraining data or as a prompt source, for example taking the first 30 tokens as prompts and generating the next 200 tokens.
