# SummEval
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 12 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, the prevailing framing is a benchmark or dataset for evaluating summary quality in text summarization. Most descriptions present human ratings on four dimensions, fluency, coherence, consistency, and relevance, typically with Likert scores from 1 to 5; one description further specifies news articles and summaries, and one paper specifies 1,600 samples annotated by three human raters. A smaller use treats it mainly as an existing benchmark for comparison on data size and critique dimensions.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use SummEval to measure text summarization, which is the only linked behavioral indicator, and no constructs are linked to it. Across the linked claims, it is attached to a single behavioral indicator rather than spread across multiple constructs, and the evaluation itself is described in terms of summary quality on fluency, coherence, consistency, and relevance.

**Space of uses.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. Papers most commonly position it as a summarization benchmark, sometimes alongside DialSumm, and one paper discusses it with NEWSROOM and HANNA as datasets for evaluating text in terms of high-level concepts; another treats it as a comparison benchmark when discussing data scale and critique dimensions.
