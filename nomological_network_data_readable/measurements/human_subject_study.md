# Human subject study
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** Community survey, User survey, Human survey, Open-ended interview  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, this label covers several direct-participant methods rather than a single fixed protocol. The prevailing usage is survey-based: papers describe a community survey to gather perspectives on harmful model behaviours and ideal responses, a mixed-methods user survey with open-ended questions and Likert ratings to identify adoption patterns, use cases, and challenges, and a questionnaire on frustrations and the perceived helpfulness of clarifications, entity graphs, and relationship graphs. A smaller line of work uses open-ended interviews to elicit detailed feedback on participants’ experience and workflow fit.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers do not report this instrument as measuring any linked constructs or behavioral indicators, and no construct or behavioral indicator is linked to it. In practice, the studies use it to elicit participants’ perspectives, frustrations, adoption patterns, perceived helpfulness, and experience in the papers’ own terms.

**Space of uses.** Most papers position human subject studies as an elicitation or validation step alongside broader system or model work, for example to broaden a list of harms, identify user challenges, understand frustrations, validate proposed features, or gather nuanced feedback about fit in a workflow. No measurement instrument is linked to it.

## Sources

> A survey of 120 TGNB community members in the USA to gather broad perspectives on harmful model behaviours and ideal model responses.

> A mixed-methods survey with open-ended questions and Likert scale ratings administered to 106 participants with visual impairments to identify adoption patterns, use cases, and challenges with AI visual assistants.

> A questionnaire study of regular text-to-image users assessing frustrations and the perceived helpfulness of clarification, entity graphs, and relationship graphs.

**[Interdisciplinary Research in Conversation: A Case Study in Computational Morphology for Language Documentation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.568.pdf) (as "Open-ended interview")**
> A 30-minute qualitative interview protocol used to elicit participants’ detailed feedback about GlossLM and its fit in documentation workflows.
