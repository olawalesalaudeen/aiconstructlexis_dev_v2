# LIWC
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** LIWC-22  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the cited papers, LIWC is described either as Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count, a psycholinguistic feature extraction tool, or as LIWC-22, a text analysis program. Definitions converge on its role in categorizing words into linguistic and psychological dimensions such as emotion, cognition, and formality, and in calculating the percentage of words in a text that fall into such categories. A narrower versioned usage names LIWC-22 specifically, while another paper refers to LIWC more generally as providing psycholinguistic characteristics.

**What it is claimed to measure.** The only reported measured_by link treats LIWC as measuring Stylistic variation, with the paper describing it as a traditional stylometric feature set that provides psycholinguistic characteristics. In these papers, it is linked to one behavioral indicator, Stylistic variation, and to no constructs, so its reported measurement use is concentrated on that single behavioral indicator rather than spread across multiple constructs.

**Space of uses.** Papers use LIWC as an analysis tool applied to text or responses. One paper reports segment comparison results using LIWC alongside WritePrint features, while another says responses were analyzed with LIWC-22 and that trait-specific linguistic features were constructed from LIWC outputs. No measurement instrument is linked to it in these papers.
