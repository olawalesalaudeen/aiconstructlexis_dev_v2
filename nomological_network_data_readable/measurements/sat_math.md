# SAT-Math
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 8 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most definitions treat SAT-Math as a standardized benchmark or dataset drawn from math problems on the SAT exam. Some papers describe it more specifically as multiple-choice questions from the SAT math section, and one description says the tasks include Intermediate Algebra, Probability, and Calculus. Papers also frame it as an evaluation resource used under standardized conditions.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link it to 1 construct and 1 behavioral indicator, so the recorded measurement claims are concentrated rather than spread across many targets. The prevailing usage is that SAT-Math measures mathematical reasoning, with papers listing it among representative math evaluation datasets. One paper also reports it as measuring mathematical problem solving.

**Space of uses.** Papers frequently place SAT-Math in math benchmark suites alongside MATH, GSM8K, AQuA-RAT or AQuA, MMLU-Math, and OCW. They position it as one component of broader evaluation, for example as part of an averaged math domain score, among five representative datasets, or simply as a benchmark on which results are reported.
