# Mean Opinion Score
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 5 papers  
**Also known as:** MOS, Mean Opinion Score (MOS), Mean Opinion Score (MOS) test  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the cited definitions, Mean Opinion Score is most commonly treated as a subjective human rating protocol on a 1 to 5, 5-point, or Likert-like scale. The prevailing usage is in speech and audio, where listeners rate synthesized speech or generated responses for naturalness or overall quality, while a smaller usage applies the same format to text, where raters judge quality and diversity.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use MOS to rate a range of output qualities rather than tying it to one named target. The recurring claims are that it gauges naturalness and quality in synthesized speech or generated audio, and quality and diversity in text, with some definitions further describing perceived fluency and coherence; however, papers do not explicitly link it to any construct or behavioral indicator.

**Space of uses.** Papers typically position MOS as a subjective human evaluation step. In the audio-focused work, it is described as a commonly used crowd-sourced listening test, including collection through Amazon Mechanical Turk, and one paper reports using MOS alongside a separate prosody mean opinion score, P-MOS, when evaluating prosody and naturalness; in the text-focused work, authors use human MOS both to gauge quality and as a separate score for diversity.
