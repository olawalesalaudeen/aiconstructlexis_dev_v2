## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the definitions, MAMI is most commonly treated as a publicly available dataset or benchmark of memes for evaluation, especially around misogyny and abusive or harmful content. The prevailing description says it contains memes that are predominantly derogatory towards women, while another recurring framing presents it as a shared-task resource for multimedia automatic misogyny identification with binary and multi-label misogyny classification. Some papers describe it more broadly as a dataset of multimodal abusive memes used to benchmark detection of abusive language and imagery in social media content.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Across reported links, papers use MAMI to measure one behavioral indicator, Harmful content detection, and it is not linked to any construct. The recorded measured_by relation says Harmful content detection is measured by MAMI, with the dataset used for evaluation in that role. The measurement claims therefore converge on a single reported target rather than being spread across multiple constructs or indicators.

**Space of uses.** In use, a paper places MAMI alongside HarM and FHM as part of a set of publicly available meme datasets for evaluation. Across the definitions, papers position it as a benchmark dataset or shared-task evaluation resource for evaluation of harmful content detection and of misogyny or abusive content in memes.
