# tinyBenchmarks
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 4 papers  
**Also known as:** TinyBenchmarks, tinybenchmarks  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, tinyBenchmarks is most commonly described as a smaller, more efficient evaluation resource for language models, variously framed as a suite of few-shot tasks, a collection of small-scale datasets for low-cost evaluation, or smaller versions of popular benchmarks for rapid evaluation. One use description specifies few-shot components including 25-shot tinyARC, 5-shot GSM8K, 10-shot tinyHellaswag, and 5-shot tinyWinoGrande.

**What it is claimed to measure.** The papers describe it as assessing model performance and enabling efficient evaluation of language models, but they do not explicitly link it to any named constructs or behavioral indicators. No measured_by relationships are recorded for the instrument, so its measurement use is stated in general terms rather than tied to a particular construct.

**Space of uses.** Papers use tinyBenchmarks as an efficiency-oriented evaluation option. One paper reports scoring models on its few-shot tasks, another says it chose the small-scale datasets "for other tasks," and another states, "We use tinyBenchmarks (Polo et al., 2024) for all the benchmarks except HumanEval to further increase the efficiency of experiments" (Speculate, then Collaborate: Fusing Knowledge of Language Models during Decoding). No other measurement instruments are linked to it.
