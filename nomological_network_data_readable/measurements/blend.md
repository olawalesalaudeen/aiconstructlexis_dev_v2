# BLEnD
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 6 papers  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, BLEnD is most commonly described as a culture-specific benchmark dataset for everyday knowledge questions across diverse cultures and languages, with data sourced from authentic, real-world scenarios in native-speaking regions. Some authors describe it through particular parts or settings, such as its short-answer questions in native languages, while another presents it as a traditional annotation setup in which annotators answered fixed questions from 500 pre-defined templates for Indonesian and Nigerian cultural knowledge. One description also expands the name as the Benchmark of Linguistic and ENcyclopedic Diversity and frames it around culturally diverse inputs.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Most descriptions frame BLEnD as testing everyday knowledge across cultures and languages. One paper describes it as designed to measure model performance on culturally diverse inputs, and another uses it to assess generalization of cultural awareness in LLMs. Papers do not explicitly link BLEnD to any constructs or behavioral indicators.

**Space of uses.** BLEnD appears as a standalone evaluation benchmark in the available papers. Reported uses include adopting and extending it when constructing a dataset, using its short-answer questions to evaluate out-of-domain generalization of culturally aligned models, and comparing model performance on the cross-cultural benchmarks WVS and BLEnD. No other measurement instrument is formally linked to it.
