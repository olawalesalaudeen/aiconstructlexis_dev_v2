# PG-19
**Type:** Measurement  
**Referenced in:** 34 papers  
**Also known as:** PG19  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, PG-19 is most commonly described as a long-context or long-form language modeling dataset or benchmark built from Project Gutenberg books. Definitions vary in detail: some papers describe long documents derived from books for evaluating extended contexts, while others describe an open-vocabulary benchmark with 11 gigabytes of text from over 28,000 Project Gutenberg books published prior to 1919, or a next-token generation and auto-regressive language modeling benchmark. Papers also refer to specific subsets and evaluation slices, including test or validation sets of 100 long books or documents, sampled long documents truncated to chosen lengths, and samples averaging 69k words.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use PG-19 to measure five constructs and one behavioral indicator, so its claimed targets are spread across several areas rather than concentrated on one. Among linked constructs, long-context understanding appears most often, followed by length generalization; smaller lines of work use it to measure continual learning, fluency, and text generation quality. It is also linked to one behavioral indicator, long-form text generation, and the surrounding snippets usually describe these evaluations through perplexity, word-level perplexity, or next-token prediction on long documents.

**Space of uses.** Across papers, PG-19 is usually used for perplexity-based evaluation on long documents, including word-level perplexity, next-token prediction, and auto-regressive language modeling, and some papers also use it for latency tests under varying context lengths, streaming or infinite-length interaction settings, or decoding speedup. It often appears as one benchmark among others rather than alone, including alongside The Pile, Wikitext-103, Wikitext-2, Needle-in-the-Haystack, and LongBench. No measurement instrument is linked to it.
