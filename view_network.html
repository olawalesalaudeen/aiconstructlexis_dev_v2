Scope and Interpretation

The AI Construct Lexis represents constructs, behavioral indicators, measurement instruments, and their relationships as described or implied in published research. Its contents were extracted primarily using large language models (LLMs), with human expert validation, from papers published at ICLR 2024, NeurIPS 2024, ICLR 2025, NAACL 2025, ACL 2025, ICML 2025, and EMNLP 2025.

Please note:
  • Descriptive, not authoritative. The Lexis documents the field's measurement assumptions, including points of agreement and disagreement. It is not a validated ontology (Borsboom et al., 2004) or nomological network (Cronbach and Meehl, 1955), and should not be treated as a definitive account of what constructs exist or how they relate to one another.
  • No endorsement. The inclusion of a construct, definition, measurement instrument, or relationship does not imply that its validity has been established or that it is endorsed by the authors.
  • Incomplete and subject to error. The Lexis reflects a subset of the published literature and is necessarily incomplete. Automated extraction, harmonization, and characterization may introduce errors, omissions, or misclassifications. Human expert validation provides evidence about the quality of these processes, but does not establish the validity of the underlying measurement claims.
Our position

The constructs used to describe AI systems, their relationships to observable behaviors, and the instruments used to measure them require careful conceptual and empirical validation. Theories and methods developed for human measurement may inform this work, but their assumptions should not be adopted without examining whether they are appropriate for AI systems.

The Lexis is intended as a starting point for this process, making the field's existing assumptions explicit so that they can be systematically examined, tested, challenged, and refined.
